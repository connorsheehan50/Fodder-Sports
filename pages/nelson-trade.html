The summary

  • The Seahawks acquired guard Quenton Nelson from the Colts on Oct. 27 for a 2027 third-round pick and a 2028 fifth-round pick, pending a physical.
  • Nelson, 30, is an eight-time Pro Bowler and the No. 6 pick of the 2018 draft who has not missed a game since 2021. He is in the final year of his contract, which makes him a rental: Seattle has no guarantee it can re-sign him, and that is part of why the price was a third and a fifth.
  • Seattle's run game averages 3.9 yards per carry, 23rd in the league, and left tackle Charles Cross is expected to miss time with an ankle injury.
  • Nelson could debut Monday night when Seattle hosts the Bears.
  • The 2–5 Colts fired general manager Chris Ballard last week. Hours later, they also traded defensive tackle DeForest Buckner to San Francisco.

What the Seattle media is saying

  • A steal. Local radio called the price a bargain for a player of Nelson's caliber.
  • A rental with a path to more. Seattle coverage points to general manager John Schneider's habit of adding veterans on expiring contracts at midseason and then signing them long-term.
  • Low risk either way. Seattle has extra picks, and if Nelson leaves in free agency, a compensatory pick could offset much of the cost.

What the Indianapolis media is saying

  • "The Colts have begun their teardown." Indianapolis coverage frames the trade as the first of many, with running back Jonathan Taylor named as the biggest asset left.
  • Colts coach Shane Steichen called the day "hard, definitely hard" and said he has "so much respect for those guys." He declined to say whether Nelson had asked for a trade.

What everyone else is saying about this trade

  • Seahawks: National coverage sees an immediate upgrade at a position of need. Nelson has only played left guard, so Grey Zabel would likely move to the right side.
  • Seahawks: With running backs Jadarian Price and Zach Charbonnet both returning, Monday night could be the first look at Seattle's full rushing attack.
  • Colts: Indianapolis had little leverage once the league knew it was selling. Some writers were surprised it could not get a second-round pick, while others noted the return beats what a compensatory pick would have brought.

The trade grades

  • Seahawks: grades ranged from B+ to A+ across seven national outlets, for an average of A. The Athletic was highest (A+); Yahoo Sports was lowest (B+).
  • Colts: grades ranged from C+ to B+, for an average of B−. The Athletic and ESPN gave B+; CBS Sports, Sports Illustrated, and Yahoo Sports gave C+.

Win-win or fleeced?

Seattle won the trade by consensus, but no national outlet called it a fleecing. Seattle-side writers did. For Indianapolis, the national view is that the Colts got a fair price for an expiring contract from a position of weakness.
