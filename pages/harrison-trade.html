The 2024 No. 4 pick heads to a 1–4 Texans team that needed a receiver, and back to his Ohio State quarterback.

The summary

  • DeMeco Ryans announced the deal at the end of his news conference after Houston's 26–3 win over Tennessee on Sunday, Oct. 11: "We traded for Marvin Harrison." Arizona made it official the next afternoon.
  • The first reports had the late-round picks the other way around. Final terms send the sixth-rounder to Houston and the seventh-rounder to Arizona.
  • Harrison reunites with C.J. Stroud, his college teammate at Ohio State, on a Houston offense that needed a second receiver next to Nico Collins.
  • In Arizona this season, Michael Wilson and Trey McBride had taken over the targets. Harrison had five catches for 85 yards and a touchdown on 13 targets and was inactive the day of the trade.
  • Over 33 games in Arizona, he had 1,578 receiving yards and 13 touchdowns.

What the Houston media is saying

  • Excited. Stroud was elated after the game and called Harrison "probably the hardest worker I've ever been around in my life."
  • The fit is the selling point. Houston gets a former top-five pick with a quarterback who already knows him, for a passing game led by a Stroud who had six touchdowns and no interceptions through five games.
  • It is also a bet on the season. The Texans are 1–4, three games behind Jacksonville in the AFC South.

What the Arizona media is saying

  • A fair return. Arizona coverage credits the front office for cutting its losses rather than selling in a panic, and highlights the national grades that liked Arizona's side.
  • Harrison never fit the new offense, in the local view, with questions about his hands and separation that followed him through his time there.
  • On Houston's side, local writers call it a gamble on a 24-year-old who was a top-five pick two years ago.

What everyone else is saying about this trade

  • Cardinals: The case for Arizona is timing. ESPN's Seth Walder argued Harrison "will be worth more to a contending team than he is to them," and his value would likely have dropped by the offseason.
  • Texans: The case for Houston is the fresh start. Harrison himself was named the biggest winner of the deal.
  • Texans: The skeptics say Houston paid for draft pedigree over production. Harrison averaged 47.8 receiving yards per game in Arizona, with two 100-yard games.

Win-win or fleeced?

Win-win, by the first day's verdict. National outlets split evenly on which side did better, and none called it a fleecing. Arizona's side averaged a slightly higher grade because it got a second-round pick for a receiver whose role had shrunk. Houston's side drew the wider range, from high marks for the reunion with Stroud to low marks for paying for pedigree.

The one outlier was The Athletic, which gave Arizona a D.
