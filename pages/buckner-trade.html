The summary

  • Hours after trading guard Quenton Nelson to Seattle, the Colts sent defensive tackle DeForest Buckner to the 49ers for a 2027 fourth-round pick. The trade was reported Oct. 27 and announced Oct. 28. San Francisco released cornerback Jack Jones to make room.
  • The 49ers drafted Buckner seventh overall in 2016 and traded him to Indianapolis in 2020 for a first-round pick.
  • Buckner, 32, has four sacks in seven games this season and is in the final year of his contract. He had 47 sacks in 95 games as a Colt and was a first-team All-Pro in 2020.
  • San Francisco's pressure rate with four or fewer rushers is 23%, second-worst in the league.
  • The 49ers are on their Week 8 bye and host the Raiders in Week 9. Pass rusher Nick Bosa is expected back from a calf injury after the bye.

What the San Francisco media is saying

  • An upgrade at a bargain price. San Francisco coverage notes the 49ers never found a lasting replacement after trading Buckner, and got him back for a fourth-round pick and the rest of his $7.9 million salary.
  • The bye week is good timing, giving Buckner time to learn defensive coordinator Raheem Morris's scheme before the Raiders game.
  • The risks: he is a rental on an expiring contract, missed 12 games over the past two seasons, and had neck surgery last year.

What the Indianapolis media is saying

  • The fire sale is in high gear. Indianapolis coverage calls Buckner a franchise cornerstone who stayed partly on the promise of leading a contender's defense.
  • "All I care about is winning," Buckner said, adding, "I have no regrets or anything about coming to Indy."
  • Colts defensive lineman Adetomiwa Adebawore, who also has four sacks, is in line to take over Buckner's spot.

What everyone else is saying about this trade

  • 49ers: National coverage sees a contender patching an injury-hit defensive line with a proven interior pass rusher. General manager John Lynch had called trading Buckner in 2020 the hardest decision of his tenure.
  • Colts: The national view is that Indianapolis got a fair return for an unhappy veteran headed for free agency. The deal leaves $10.8 million in dead money on the Colts' cap.

The trade grades

  • 49ers: an A− from all three national outlets that graded it (ESPN, NFL.com, and Yahoo Sports).
  • Colts: grades ranged from B− to B+, for an average of B. NFL.com was highest (B+).

Win-win or fleeced?

Win-win. San Francisco gets a proven starter for a mid-round pick, and Indianapolis gets value for a player who was likely to leave after the season. Nobody called it a fleecing.
