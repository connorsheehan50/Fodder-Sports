The summary

  • The Patriots (4-3) lost 30-24 at Chicago on Thursday Night Football, committing 10 penalties and missing 17 tackles. That ended a three-game win streak.
  • The Dolphins (1-5) won their first game of the season, 28-25 at the Jets. It was Hafley's first win as an NFL head coach.
  • Brown (ankle/knee) and Patriots running back TreVeyon Henderson (illness) were downgraded to out. Only three wide receivers remain on New England's 53-man roster: DeMario Douglas, Kyle Williams, and Efton Chism III.
  • Maye has 14 touchdown passes and two interceptions over his last four games. Miami's pass defense ranks last in passer rating allowed (116.8) and last in sacks with six.
  • Miami has run for 100 or more yards in five straight games behind Dolphins running back Ollie Gordon II. New England's run defense ranks eighth, allowing 95.3 yards per game.
  • New England has 14 giveaways and has not won the turnover battle in any game. The Patriots are 18-43 all-time in South Florida.

What the New England media is saying about the Patriots

  • Uneasy. Boston coverage likes Maye's form but calls the team sloppy, pointing to 25 missed tackles over two weeks and the worst passer rating allowed in zone coverage. Patriots coach Mike Vrabel said, "The tackling wasn't good enough, not against this team."
  • Brown's knee is the biggest worry. New England traded a 2028 first-round pick for him in June, and he has nine catches for 101 yards in three games. Brown had said, "None of that stuff even matters. I'm out here, so I'm going."
  • Patriots tight ends Hunter Henry and Eli Raridon are the bright spot, with 29 catches, 361 yards, and three touchdowns over four games against a Miami defense that struggles with tight ends.
  • Patriots cornerback Carlton Davis III returns from a neck injury in his hometown. He said, "So, it's a full-circle moment for me."

What the Miami media is saying about the Patriots

  • Miami's media sees New England as the better team, crediting a defense that ranks 10th in yards allowed and a pass rush led by Patriots linebacker Elijah Ponder's 5.5 sacks.
  • It also sees cracks: Maye leads the league with eight interceptions, and coverage calls the Patriots a little uneven. Brown and Henderson's absences are framed as a break for Miami.
  • Dolphins players speak with respect. Dolphins edge Chop Robinson said of Maye, "He's a great quarterback."

What the Miami media is saying about the Dolphins

  • Encouraged. Miami's coverage calls the Jets win a big deal for a young team, praising Dolphins quarterback Malik Willis (208 yards, two passing touchdowns, and a rushing score) and the physical run game.
  • The local focus is growth, not the standings. Hafley said, "Division games are important, and I think it's important for a young team to understand that."
  • Injuries on the line and in the secondary are the main worries. Dolphins guard Kadyn Proctor (concussion) is out, and cornerback JuJu Brents is questionable after concussion protocol.
  • Willis facing Vrabel, his former Titans coach, is a storyline. Willis said, "You play the team, you don't play the coach, you know what I mean?"

What the New England media is saying about the Dolphins

  • Boston's media sees a rebuilding roster with an NFL-high 15 rookies, a pass defense that ranks 30th in yards per attempt allowed, and a quarterback with a 77.1 passer rating.
  • It respects Miami's effort and running game, which has averaged 131.5 rushing yards over the last three games despite losing running back De'Von Achane to a torn ACL.
  • Patriots players are not overlooking Miami. Vrabel said, "They play extremely hard. I have a lot of respect for what Jeff's done."

What everyone else is saying about this game

  • Patriots: National coverage treats New England as a top AFC contender, crediting Maye and a passing game that ranks fifth in dropback EPA.
  • Patriots: Brown's injury drew wide national attention, with coverage noting he averages 8.3 yards per catch since returning, against 15.3 for his career.
  • Patriots: National coverage also flags the Patriots' 14 giveaways and their history of strange losses in Miami.
  • Dolphins: Miami is seen as an improving rebuild that is not ready for a contender, though Hafley drew praise for the win in New York and his fourth-down call to seal it.
  • Dolphins: The Dolphins' run game and Robinson's pass rush are the national bright spots, with Robinson ranking second among edge rushers in pass rush win rate.

Who's out

  • Out: Patriots wide receiver A.J. Brown (ankle/knee), running back TreVeyon Henderson (illness), and offensive tackle Marcus Bryant (ankle, not activated from injured reserve). Brown and Henderson were downgraded from questionable Saturday.
  • Questionable: Patriots linebacker Christian Elliss (chest) and cornerback Carlton Davis III (neck).
  • Out: Dolphins guard/tackle Kadyn Proctor (concussion).
  • Questionable: Dolphins cornerbacks JuJu Brents (neck/concussion) and Reese Taylor (quad). Both were full participants Friday.
