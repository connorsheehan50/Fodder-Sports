The summary

  • The Bears (5-2) traded their 2027 and 2028 first-round picks and a 2027 fourth to Tennessee for All-Pro defensive tackle Jeffery Simmons on Oct. 29, getting a 2028 fifth back. Simmons is signed through 2030 and is expected to debut Monday.
  • The Seahawks (4-3) sent a 2027 third and a 2028 fifth to Indianapolis for guard Quenton Nelson on Oct. 27. He starts at left guard, and Simmons and Nelson have faced each other twice a year in the AFC South since 2019.
  • Seattle beat previously unbeaten Kansas City 20-3 on Oct. 25, holding the Chiefs to 213 yards and no red-zone trips. Its defense leads the NFL in yards allowed (233.9 per game) and has not allowed a 100-yard rusher in 36 straight games.
  • Chicago leads the NFL in rushing at 154.3 yards per game. Quarterback Caleb Williams has topped 300 passing yards in both games since returning from a hamstring injury, including 308 yards and two touchdowns in the 30-24 win over New England.
  • Seahawks wide receiver Jaxon Smith-Njigba leads the NFL with 867 receiving yards and eight touchdowns, against a Bears pass defense that allows a league-high 8.7 yards per attempt.
  • Seattle will be without left tackle Charles Cross and wide receiver Rashid Shaheed. Chicago is without running back Kyle Monangai again, while defensive end Montez Sweat returns from a two-game suspension.

What the Chicago media is saying about the Bears

  • All in. Chicago coverage treats the Simmons trade as the end of general manager Ryan Poles' rebuild and the start of a real Super Bowl chase, and as a sign the Bears believe Williams has arrived as a franchise quarterback.
  • The building is giddy. Defensive coordinator Dennis Allen called the trade "like waking up on Christmas morning," and Simmons said, "This team believes in what they have here and they want it right now."
  • The caution is about the cost. Chicago's media weighs two first-round picks, rising cap hits, and the memory of the 2018 Khalil Mack trade, though it argues this time the Bears waited until they had their quarterback.
  • The worry for Monday is the secondary. Cornerback Jaylon Johnson has allowed a 113.1 passer rating on throws his way, and Chicago sees Smith-Njigba as the toughest test of the year.

What the Seattle media is saying about the Bears

  • Seattle's media respects the Bears' offense: Williams is described as a dangerous, mobile passer who is growing into the quarterback everyone expected, and Ben Johnson's scheme is treated as a serious challenge.
  • It is skeptical of Chicago's defense, noting the Bears allow 6.6 yards per play, last in the NFL, and a league-high 8.7 yards per pass attempt.
  • Simmons is seen as a real upgrade for that front, and Seattle notes he arrives just as Nelson does. Seahawks coach Mike Macdonald said, "We've got a lot of respect for him."

What the Seattle media is saying about the Seahawks

  • Confident. Seattle's media calls the Kansas City win a statement that showed the champions' playoff form after a three-losses-in-four stretch.
  • The defense is the pride of the city. Seattle allows 4.0 yards per play, and Macdonald called his defenders "a bunch of warriors."
  • The Nelson trade is cheered as another of general manager John Schneider's midseason moves, following Leonard Williams, Ernest Jones IV, and Shaheed. Safety Julian Love called Nelson "a walking Hall of Famer."
  • The concerns are on offense: quarterback Sam Darnold leads the NFL with eight interceptions, the line is reshuffled without Cross, and the red zone produced six points on three trips against Kansas City.

What the Chicago media is saying about the Seahawks

  • Chicago's media sees Seattle's defense as the best in football. Williams said so himself, and the Mahomes game is described as a humiliation of the Chiefs.
  • It calls Smith-Njigba the NFL's best receiver, on pace for 2,106 yards. Bears coach Ben Johnson called him "the engine" of Seattle's offense.
  • The opening Chicago sees is Darnold's turnovers. Seattle is 0-3 when its offense commits three or more turnovers, and the Bears are 3-0 when they force three.

What everyone else is saying about this game

  • Bears: National coverage calls the Simmons deal a franchise-altering swing: he is the first defensive tackle since 1967 traded for multiple first-round picks, and he leads all defensive tackles with 28 pressures.
  • Bears: Grades were positive but measured, praising the upgrade while calling the cost steep, and noting Chicago's defense has looked better in totals than per play.
  • Seahawks: Seattle's win over Kansas City drew rave reviews, with Smith-Njigba's 150 yards and a defense that kept a Patrick Mahomes offense out of the red zone for the first time.
  • Seahawks: The Nelson trade is viewed as a sign Seattle is serious about repeating, while Darnold's eight interceptions remain the national question mark.
  • Bears: The Bears are No. 7 in our consensus power rankings, and the Seahawks are No. 5.

Who's out

  • Out: Bears running back Kyle Monangai (thumb, toe). Fourth straight missed game.
  • Out for season: Bears tight end Cole Kmet (knee). On injured reserve.
  • Questionable: Bears cornerback Malik Muhammad (toe) and linebacker D'Marco Jackson (ankle).
  • In: Bears tight end Colston Loveland (neck), linebacker T.J. Edwards (hamstring), and linebacker Jack Sanborn (concussion) have no designation.
  • Out: Seahawks left tackle Charles Cross (ankle), wide receiver Rashid Shaheed (knee), and running back Velus Jones Jr. (hamstring).
  • Doubtful: Seahawks linebacker Uchenna Nwosu (ankle).
  • Questionable: Seahawks quarterback Drew Lock (right elbow), guard Anthony Bradford (knee), defensive tackle Brandon Pili (back), and linebacker Patrick O'Connell (toe).
  • In: Seahawks running back Zach Charbonnet (knee) is set to make his season debut. Running back Jadarian Price (chest) is on injured reserve, and his activation has not been confirmed.
