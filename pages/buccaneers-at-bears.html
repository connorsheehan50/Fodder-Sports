The summary

  • The Bears (6-2) have won three straight, capped by a 34-17 win over the defending champion Seahawks on Monday night. A win would give Ben Johnson more wins than George Halas has on the Bears' all-time coaching list, trailing only Ralph Jones.
  • The Buccaneers (3-5) have won three of four after an 0-4 start. They beat Atlanta 16-0 in a storm with a team-record 324 rushing yards and no completed passes, and they lead the NFL at 168.6 rushing yards per game.
  • Bucs running backs Bucky Irving (728 yards) and Kenneth Gainwell carry the offense, while Tampa Bay ranks last in passing at 131.4 yards per game. Quarterback Baker Mayfield has a right thumb injury that the Chicago Tribune says may not fully heal until after the season.
  • Bears defensive tackle Jeffery Simmons, acquired from Tennessee on Oct. 29, had a sack, six tackles, and drew 13 double teams in his debut. Bucs coach Todd Bowles and Mayfield both singled him out this week.
  • Bears quarterback Caleb Williams is completing about 71% of his passes, second in the NFL, and Chicago leads the league in rushing touchdowns. Tampa Bay's defense leads the NFL in interceptions and is tied for the fewest rushing touchdowns allowed.
  • Chicago has a bye after this game. The Bucs are tied with Atlanta, half a game behind Carolina in the NFC South.

What the Tampa Bay media is saying about the Buccaneers

  • Encouraged. Tampa coverage loves the new identity: the offensive line of Graham Barton, Ben Bredeson, and Cody Mauch is opening holes, and Irving and Gainwell, whom Bowles calls a "1A, 1B" pair, just set the team rushing record.
  • The defense gets just as much credit, with 10 sacks and nine interceptions since Week 5 and a league-high 12 interceptions overall. Coverage that questioned Bowles' job earlier this season now credits him for the turnaround.
  • The worry is the passing game. The Bucs went 15 straight drives without a completion, Mayfield's thumb is still an issue, and Tampa writers are skeptical of Bowles' talk of winning 9-3 games "from a field goal standpoint."
  • Tampa's media treats Sunday as a measuring stick and the toughest test of the season, and expects the Bucs to need more than the run in clear weather in Chicago.

What the Chicago media is saying about the Buccaneers

  • Chicago coverage respects the run game but doubts the rest. It points out that the Bucs have scored 20 or fewer points five times and cannot win if they have to throw.
  • Chicago writers call Tampa Bay's defensive numbers inflated by weak offenses and bad weather, while conceding Bowles is a crafty play caller. Some flag the matchup as a possible trap game.
  • Bears defensive coordinator Dennis Allen gave the Bucs their due: "They're running the ball better than anybody else in the league right now."

What the Chicago media is saying about the Bears

  • Fired up. Chicago coverage is full of contender talk after the Seattle win, the best start since 2012, and the team beating both of last season's Super Bowl teams in one season for the first time.
  • Williams gets most of the praise. Since returning from his hamstring injury he has completed 73.8% for 860 yards, four touchdowns, and no interceptions, and he calls the Bears "a smashmouth football team."
  • Simmons is already treated as a difference maker. Allen says he "raises the level of everybody on the field," and his presence freed defensive end Montez Sweat for one-on-one rushes.
  • The concerns are depth and the run game. Running back Kyle Monangai has missed four games with turf toe, backup Zavier Scott went to injured reserve, and Chicago has gained no more than 3.5 yards per carry in any of the last three games despite running back D'Andre Swift's 10 rushing touchdowns.

What the Tampa Bay media is saying about the Bears

  • Tampa's media calls the Bears one of the NFL's most complete teams, with the third-ranked offense, the top third-down defense, and the league lead in time of possession.
  • Bucs writers see openings in Williams' 7.6% sack rate, the Bears' seven lost fumbles, and a Chicago secondary whose cornerbacks grade near the bottom in coverage.
  • The key matchup in Tampa coverage is Ben Johnson against Bowles. Bowles called Johnson's offense hard to prepare for: "He slows the tempo, he speeds the tempo... He uses everybody in the offense."

What everyone else is saying about this game

  • Bears: National coverage now treats Chicago as a serious contender after the win in Seattle, with credit spread across Johnson's coaching, Williams, and Simmons.
  • Bears: National writers credit Simmons and Williams for the Seattle win: Williams threw for 247 yards with no turnovers, and Seattle went 1 of 11 on third down.
  • Buccaneers: The 16-0 win drew national attention as one of the strangest of recent seasons: the Bucs were the first team since the 1974 Bills to finish with zero completions.
  • Buccaneers: National views of the Bucs split. Some see a ground game that can wear down contenders and carry Tampa Bay to a division title, while others say they are not believers until Mayfield throws again.
  • Buccaneers: National coverage flags Simmons against Bucs center Graham Barton as a key matchup, and notes the Bears have not allowed a 100-yard rusher since Week 2.

Who's out

  • Out: Bucs outside linebacker Yaya Diaby (knee).
  • Questionable: Bucs wide receiver Emeka Egbuka (foot). He was a full participant Thursday.
  • In: Bucs tackle Tristan Wirfs (toe) and quarterback Baker Mayfield (right thumb) carry no game status. Wirfs was limited Friday after missing Thursday.
  • Out: Bears running back Zavier Scott (thumb) is on injured reserve.
  • Questionable: Bears running back Kyle Monangai (toe), cornerback Tyrique Stevenson (thumb), and cornerback Malik Muhammad (toe). Monangai practiced for the first time in over a month, but Chicago coverage expects his return after the bye.
