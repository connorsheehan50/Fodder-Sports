The Summary

  • The Bears are 6-2 and coming off a 34-17 Monday Night blowout in Seattle. Caleb Williams completed 24 of 33 passes. Jeffery Simmons had a sack and two pressures in his debut.
  • The Buccaneers are 3-5 but their defense just produced one of the most remarkable games in NFL history: zero completions allowed, a team-record 324 rushing yards. The offense has been inconsistent all season.
  • The line opened Chicago -6, total 47.5 (DraftKings via ESPN, Nov. 6). Pickers back the Bears at 87%, with an average score of Bears 28, Buccaneers 18.
  • Series history: the Bears lead 40-22. The last meeting was 2023, Buccaneers 27-17 in Tampa Bay.

What the Tampa Bay Media Is Saying About the Buccaneers

  • Belief in the defense. Tampa Bay coverage spent the week dissecting the Week 8 shutout — the first time in NFL history a team allowed zero completions — and treating it as proof that this defense is elite.
  • Skeptical about the offense. The Buccaneers' passing attack has not matched the defense's level for most of the season, and coverage acknowledges the matchup in Chicago is a steep ask.

What the Chicago Media Is Saying About the Bears

  • Fired up and focused. The post-Seattle coverage was celebratory, and the preview coverage pivoted quickly: the Bears have a game to win on Sunday Night Football, not a party to attend.
  • Simmons is the story. Every Bears preview angle runs through the new defensive tackle. What can he do against Baker Mayfield? Can he replicate what he did in his debut?

What the National Media Is Saying

  • Bears in a tough spot. National coverage likes Chicago but notes that the Buccaneers' defense is dangerous enough to slow anyone. The game is treated as a credibility test for the Bears before the final push.
  • Baker Mayfield gets attention. After the Week 8 shutout, national writers are treating Mayfield as the x-factor. Can he replicate that outcome against one of the AFC's best defenses?

Injuries

  • Buccaneers (official report): Yaya Diaby Out; Emeka Egbuka Questionable.
  • Bears (NFL.com report): Kyle Monangai Questionable, Tyrique Stevenson Questionable, Malik Muhammad Questionable; Zavier Scott IR.
