Both teams are 5-2, and the winner leaves Sunday alone atop the AFC North after a week of trash talk.

Cleveland coverage is the warmest it has been in years: the young Browns are winning close games under Todd Monken, and the market sees a team that has outgrown its history. The summary

  • Both teams are 5-2. Cleveland holds the AFC North lead on the tiebreaker after a 27-24 Week 4 win, sealed by Andre Szmyt's 56-yard field goal with 10 seconds left.
  • The Browns have lost 22 straight regular-season games in Pittsburgh, last winning there in 2003. A win would give them their first season sweep of the Steelers since 1988.
  • Every Browns win has come on a game-winning score late, but quarterback Deshaun Watson has been sacked a league-high 25 times. Steelers quarterback Aaron Rodgers is second with 22.
  • Pittsburgh has won three straight, including a 16-10 win over the Saints in Paris, and has a bye next week. Its defense allows 19.7 points per game, fifth in the NFL, but the offense has one rushing touchdown all season.
  • Browns defensive end Jared Verse called the Steelers 'dirty' after the first game. Steelers defensive tackle Cam Heyward answered that the comments 'will be addressed' on Sunday.
  • Browns right tackle Tytus Howard (concussion) is questionable after missing practice all week, though he has been reported as cleared. The Steelers are without cornerback Brandin Echols and defensive back Doneiko Slaughter, which thins the secondary behind the starters.

What the Cleveland media is saying about the Browns

  • Believers. Cleveland's media sees a young team that does not carry the franchise's baggage, and frames a win in Pittsburgh as the moment the rest of the league has to take the Browns seriously.
  • The defense is the source of the confidence: first in the NFL in tackles for loss (46), five sacks and two interceptions of Rodgers in Week 4, and a defensive line at its healthiest since the first meeting with Mason Graham and Mike Hall Jr. back.
  • The worries are early holes and the run defense. The Browns have been outscored badly in first quarters, and they have allowed more 10-yard runs than any team, which matters against Steelers running back Jaylen Warren.
  • Verse's trash talk splits the room. Some see it as the nasty edge this team needed, others call it a bad look for a pass rusher who still has no sacks.

What the Pittsburgh media is saying about the Browns

  • Pittsburgh's media respects the Browns' defense and their clutch finishes, but sees a fragile offense: league-worst sack rate, a 36% third-down rate, and six lost fumbles.
  • Verse's comments are being used as bulletin-board material in the Steelers' locker room, and the coverage expects Cleveland's run of last-minute wins to end at some point.
  • There is real credit for the future, with Browns rookies like wide receiver KC Concepcion and linebacker Carson Schwesinger seen as problems for years to come.

What the Pittsburgh media is saying about the Steelers

  • Confident. Pittsburgh coverage sees a team rolling on three straight wins, the AFC's top overall defense, and a run game that just put up a season-high 148 yards in Paris.
  • Running back Jaylen Warren (561 rushing yards, 855 from scrimmage) is the engine, with Rico Dowdle back from injury, and linebacker Payton Wilson is being called on track for his best season.
  • The concerns are red zone offense, thin depth at cornerback with two players out, and Rodgers taking sacks on third down.
  • Players frame the Week 4 loss as a lesson in handling adversity and want to go 4-0 in this stretch before the bye.

What the Cleveland media is saying about the Steelers

  • Cleveland's media treats Pittsburgh's defense as one of the league's best, especially at home, and sees Warren and Dowdle as the main threat to a shaky Browns run defense.
  • It is skeptical of the rest: Rodgers is seen as having lost some zip, the line has let him down at times, and the Steelers' three-game streak came against teams that are each 2-5.
  • Some Browns coverage sees a hidden edge in the Steelers' long flight home from Paris.

What everyone else is saying about this game

  • Browns: National coverage credits Monken for turning Watson into a steady late-game passer, but notes the Browns are the only AFC North team with a negative point differential and rank 28th in scoring.
  • Browns: The rivalry history leads most national previews: Pittsburgh has won 22 straight regular-season home games against Cleveland, second-longest such streak in NFL history.
  • Steelers: Pittsburgh is seen as the steadier team, with a top-five scoring defense and the better quarterback, but its offense ranks 27th in red zone scoring.
  • Steelers: Rodgers' 540th career touchdown pass moved him into third all-time, though national writers see too many flaws for a deep run.

Who's out

  • Questionable: Browns offensive tackle Tytus Howard (concussion). Missed all three practices; reported cleared to play.
  • Questionable: Browns safety Daniel Thomas (ankle) and cornerback Myles Harden (hamstring).
  • In: Browns defensive end Jared Verse (knee), center Elgton Jenkins (knee), safety Grant Delpit (neck), and defensive tackles Mason Graham and Mike Hall Jr. (ankles) finished the week without a game status.
  • Out: Steelers cornerback Brandin Echols (hamstring) and defensive back Doneiko Slaughter (hamstring).
  • Questionable: Steelers wide receiver Ben Skowronek (calf).
  • In: Steelers cornerback Jalen Ramsey (wrist) and linebacker Alex Highsmith (groin) were full participants Friday with no status.
