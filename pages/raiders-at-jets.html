The summary

  • The Raiders (3-4) have lost four straight after a 3-0 start, most recently 35-21 to the Rams, and have allowed 30 or more points in each loss.
  • Raiders coach Klint Kubiak says there is "no controversy" at quarterback: 38-year-old Kirk Cousins starts, and No. 1 overall pick Fernando Mendoza "is going to play at some point, but it's not going to be this week." Cousins leads the NFL with 16 touchdown passes and has 1,877 yards and five interceptions.
  • The Jets (2-5) lost 28-25 at home to previously winless Miami. Jets coach Aaron Glenn is 5-19 overall and 0-8 against the AFC East, and the Jets own the NFL's longest playoff drought at 15 years.
  • Jets quarterback Geno Smith faces the team he played for in 2025. He is completing 70% of his passes with 10 touchdowns, two interceptions, and a 105.2 rating.
  • The matchup on the ground favors New York on paper: the Raiders run for 74.7 yards per game (31st), Raiders running back Ashton Jeanty averages 3.1 yards per carry, and Las Vegas allows 143.6 rushing yards per game. Jeanty stirred things up by saying some Jets defenders "don't really want to tackle."
  • The Jets are without defensive lineman T'Vondre Sweat, edge rusher Joseph Ossai, and offensive lineman Max Mitchell, and rookie edge rusher David Bailey, their sack leader with 5.5, is doubtful.

What the Las Vegas media is saying about the Raiders

  • Frustrated. Las Vegas coverage says the 3-0 start came against weak teams, and a fifth straight loss would all but end the playoff hopes and could push the Raiders to sell before the Nov. 10 deadline.
  • The run game is the biggest worry. The Raiders managed 48 rushing yards against the Rams, and Raiders center Tyler Linderbaum summed up the fix as "Block better."
  • The defense is the other problem: 199 rushing yards allowed to the Rams, a pass rush that has four sacks in the last three games, and Raiders defensive end Maxx Crosby without a sack since Week 4.
  • The Mendoza meter keeps climbing. Local coverage is divided between wanting the rookie now and worrying that the weak run game would leave him exposed. Crosby's message: "You can't lose confidence in who you are because of noise."

What the New York media is saying about the Raiders

  • New York's media respects the Raiders' passing game. Glenn said Cousins is "tough to stop" once he gets going, and Jets linebacker Demario Davis called Raiders tight end Brock Bowers "one of the top tight ends in our game, hands down."
  • It sees a Raiders defense that can be attacked, allowing 28.1 points and 373.7 yards per game, and a run game ranked 31st.
  • Jeanty's tackling comment was shrugged off. Davis called it "bulletin board material," and Jets safety Minkah Fitzpatrick said he gives "110 percent effort, no matter who I'm playing against."

What the New York media is saying about the Jets

  • Torn. New York coverage is excited about young players, led by rookie wide receiver Omar Cooper Jr., who has back-to-back 100-yard games with touchdowns, and running back Braelon Allen, who has 357 rushing yards in four starts.
  • The Miami loss stung. Local voices call it a new low for the Glenn era and debate whether he can save his job, though the market expects him to finish the season.
  • Smith's accuracy is a positive, but the offense still struggles to finish. Jets wide receiver Garrett Wilson had three targets against Miami, and Glenn said, "We all have to get better, from coaching to playing to everything that we're doing."
  • The long view is hopeful: the Jets own three 2027 first-round picks, including Indianapolis's, which could climb as the Colts sell.

What the Las Vegas media is saying about the Jets

  • Las Vegas's media sees a much more competitive Jets team, with four of its five losses by one score and a pass defense ranked fifth.
  • It expects Smith to be hard to rattle behind a talented line led by Jets right tackle Armand Membou, though it notes Smith has taken 21 sacks.
  • The Jets' injuries at defensive line and edge are seen as a real opening, especially with running back Breece Hall and guard Dylan Parham possibly returning to boost the Jets' run game.

What everyone else is saying about this game

  • Raiders: National coverage says Cousins has been the least of the Raiders' problems. Since Week 4, Las Vegas allows 35.8 points and 416.5 yards per game.
  • Raiders: The Raiders are only the third team in 10 seasons to start 3-0 or better and then lose four straight, and the Mendoza debate is a national story.
  • Jets: Glenn's job is the national Jets story. National insiders expect him to finish the season, while the 0-8 division record and late-game failures drive the doubts.
  • Jets: The Jets' run defense has slipped to 31st since Week 3, allowing 153 yards per game, and it loses Sweat to a knee injury.
  • Raiders: The Raiders are No. 21 in our consensus power rankings, and the Jets are No. 28.

Who's out

  • Out: Raiders defensive end Patrick Johnson (stinger) and tight end Carter Runyon (foot).
  • Questionable: Raiders wide receiver Jack Bech (forearm), activated from injured reserve Oct. 31, and guard Jackson Powers-Johnson (back), the listed starter at right guard.
  • In: Raiders tight end Brock Bowers (calf), defensive tackle Folorunso Fatukasi (shoulder), tackle DJ Glaze (ankle), cornerback Eric Stokes (illness), tight end Ian Thomas (shoulder), and linebacker Quay Walker (chest) have no game status.
  • Out: Jets defensive lineman T'Vondre Sweat (knee), edge rusher Joseph Ossai (ankle), and offensive lineman Max Mitchell (concussion). Sweat was moved to injured reserve.
  • Doubtful: Jets edge rusher David Bailey (groin). He did not practice Friday.
  • Questionable: Jets running back Breece Hall (quad) and offensive lineman Dylan Parham (knee). Both were limited Friday.
  • In: Jets running back Braelon Allen (ankle) has no game status after a full practice.

Game statuses from the teams' official injury reports, Friday, Oct. 30. Bech's activation is from the Raiders' Oct. 31 transaction.
