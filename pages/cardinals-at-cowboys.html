The summary

  • Arizona (2-5) has lost five of six, including a 30-16 home loss to Denver. Dallas (3-4) beat the Eagles 38-21 in Philadelphia as Cowboys running back Javonte Williams ran for a career-high 155 yards. Arizona is No. 29 and Dallas No. 20 in our consensus power rankings.
  • History is the storyline. Arizona has won four straight in the series and is 4-0 at AT&T Stadium. Cowboys receiver CeeDee Lamb has never beaten Arizona and said Dallas has been too relaxed. Coach Brian Schottenheimer disagreed: "So the ball is what this game is going to come down to."
  • Turnovers and the red zone decide it. Dallas has no turnovers in its three wins and at least one in each loss, and it is minus-11 in turnovers against Arizona since 2008. Arizona ranks last in red-zone touchdown rate (42.9%) and went 1-for-10 in the red zone over its last two games.
  • Cardinals running back Jeremiyah Love, the No. 3 overall pick, returns from an ankle injury. Defensive lineman Walter Nolen III and guard Jon Gaines II are out, and center Hjalte Froholdt is questionable.
  • The trade deadline is Nov. 10. Arizona is framed as a possible seller, and reports have linked Dallas to Cardinals linebacker Josh Sweat.
  • Cardinals tight end Trey McBride leads the NFL with 59 catches. He faces a Dallas defense that has given up a score on the opening drive in each of its last three games.

What the Arizona media is saying about the Cardinals

  • Gloomy. Arizona coverage is stuck on the red zone: the Cardinals are last in the league in touchdown rate there and failed on four tries from Denver's 1-yard line late in Week 7. Coach Mike LaFleur took the blame: "Again, I'll look in the mirror first and say it starts with me."
  • The quarterback debate is loud. Quarterback Jacoby Brissett will start after taking 13 hits against Denver, but local coverage argues a switch to rookie quarterback Carson Beck is coming. LaFleur would only say, "Not going to talk about role changes right now."
  • Arizona's media calls Dallas an early crossroads for LaFleur in his first season. It points to an injury-hit line without left tackle Paris Johnson Jr., a slow year from receiver Marvin Harrison Jr., a weak defense, and a team trending toward selling Sweat, safety Budda Baker, or its veteran quarterbacks.
  • There are bright spots. McBride is near franchise records, Love is back, and defensive lineman Darius Robinson already matches last season's pressure total: "I have nothing to lose, so I'm playing so free and I'm having so much fun."

What the Dallas media is saying about the Cardinals

  • Dallas coverage sees a struggling team and a possible seller, but it calls this a trap game because Arizona has owned the series, winning eight of the last nine meetings.
  • Dallas's media respects the Cardinals' offense. Brissett leads the NFL with 282 pass attempts, McBride is the focus, and Cardinals defensive coordinator Nick Rallis's disguised blitzes are the concern for the Cowboys' line.
  • It also sees openings: Arizona ranks 23rd against the run, has the second-lowest pressure rate on third-and-long, and allows 10.9 yards per pass attempt in those spots.

What the Dallas media is saying about the Cowboys

  • Relieved. Dallas coverage loved the win in Philadelphia, praising Schottenheimer's play calls after a week of criticism. Quarterback Dak Prescott said his coach was "hungry and pissed off," and Prescott passed Roger Staubach for second-most wins by a Cowboys quarterback (86).
  • The run game has Dallas excited. The Cowboys topped 200 yards both rushing and passing, every offensive lineman got a game ball, and Schottenheimer said, "I thought the offensive line was just dynamite all night."
  • The defense is the worry. Opponents have scored on their opening drive in each of the last three games, third downs remain a problem, and defensive tackle Kenny Clark summed it up: "We fighting for consistency."
  • History hangs over the week. Local coverage blames self-inflicted mistakes for the Arizona losses, with 11 giveaways and one takeaway across the last five, and sees a soft stretch ahead: the next five opponents are a combined 13-22.

What the Arizona media is saying about the Cowboys

  • Arizona coverage sees a Dallas offense that is among the league's best when it clicks, coming off 38 points in Philadelphia, paired with a defense it calls porous.
  • The Cardinals' team site singles out new Cowboys cornerback Joey Porter Jr., who has allowed an 18.2% completion rate, Lamb's 93 receiving yards per game, and kicker Brandon Aubrey.
  • Arizona's media enjoyed Lamb's admission that the Cardinals "punched us in the mouth every single time," and it notes Dallas has never beaten Arizona at AT&T Stadium.

What everyone else is saying about this game

  • Cardinals: National coverage treats Arizona as one of the league's weaker teams. The Cardinals are last in scoring defense (29.7 points per game), and Brissett is 3-16 as Arizona's starter across two seasons.
  • Cardinals: National writers credit McBride and the Cardinals' long, clock-eating drives, but they see a passing game that cannot finish: Brissett has 1,600 yards, 10 touchdowns, and six interceptions.
  • Cowboys: National coverage says the Eagles win put Dallas back in the NFC East hunt, behind the 4-2-1 Giants. It still notes questions about the defense and Schottenheimer's 9-13-1 record.
  • Cowboys: Lamb's admission about Arizona became a national story, and the national framing is that Dallas has to stop beating itself with turnovers.
  • Cowboys: National analysis flags slow defensive starts and says Dallas cannot treat a 2-5 opponent as a soft landing.

Who's out

  • Out: Cardinals defensive lineman Walter Nolen III (ankle) and guard Jon Gaines II (ankle). Hayden Conner is the next man up on the line.
  • Questionable: Cardinals center Hjalte Froholdt (knee), cornerback Max Melton (knee), and safety Andrew Wingard (knee). Froholdt has not missed a game since 2023.
  • In: Cardinals running back Jeremiyah Love (ankle) returns after missing two games. Receiver Michael Wilson (ribs) will play.
  • Out for season: Cardinals left tackle Paris Johnson Jr. (torn biceps) and receiver Jalen Brooks (wrist).
  • Out: Cowboys receiver KaVontae Turpin (ribs) and linebacker Dee Winters (calf).
  • Questionable: Cowboys receiver George Pickens (shoulder, hamstring), defensive tackles Kenny Clark (back) and Jonathan Bullard (calf), linebacker Marist Liufau (forearm), safety P.J. Locke (foot), and tight end Luke Schoonmaker (groin). Schottenheimer expects Pickens to play.
  • In: Cowboys defensive tackle Quinnen Williams (hamstring) and safety Caleb Downs (ankle) practiced fully Friday.

Cowboys statuses come from the team's official injury report, Friday, Oct. 30. Cardinals statuses come from the Friday, Oct. 30 report as published by ArizonaSports.com, and Schoonmaker's listing comes from that same report.
