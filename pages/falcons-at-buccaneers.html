The summary

  • The Falcons (3-4) beat the previously unbeaten 49ers 29-20, forcing four turnovers and committing none. A win in Tampa gives them sole possession of first place in the NFC South after Carolina lost to Green Bay.
  • The Buccaneers (2-5) routed Carolina 43-13 in Charlotte with 258 rushing yards, four takeaways, and five sacks. They are still 0-4 at home, and all five losses came by one possession against teams above .500.
  • Falcons quarterback Michael Penix Jr. was named NFC Offensive Player of the Week and has nine touchdown passes and one interception in five starts since returning from his third ACL tear. He grew up in Dade City, about 40 miles from Raymond James Stadium.
  • Strength meets strength on the ground: Tampa Bay runs for 146.4 yards a game and allows 77.3, while Atlanta runs for 139.9 and allows 88.9. Atlanta's run game has slipped to 255 yards and no rushing touchdowns over its last three games.
  • Buccaneers quarterback Baker Mayfield returned from a dislocated right thumb but threw only 11 passes at Carolina, and Tampa ranks last in the NFL in passing at 150.4 yards a game. He faces Falcons coach Kevin Stefanski, his former coach in Cleveland, and said he hasn't spoken to him since 2021.
  • Falcons tight end Kyle Pitts had 11 catches, 166 yards, and three touchdowns in last December's 29-28 win in Tampa, when Atlanta came back from 14 points down in the fourth quarter. Tampa Bay ranks in the bottom 10 against tight ends.

What the Atlanta media is saying about the Falcons

  • Believers, carefully. Atlanta coverage has swung toward calling Penix the franchise quarterback, pointing to 31 points a game in his five starts, no interceptions in four games, and safety Jessie Bates III saying, "I really, truly believe that he's a franchise quarterback."
  • The run game is the worry. After 724 rushing yards in four games, Atlanta has 255 in its last three, and running back Bijan Robinson is held up as the fix: "That's our identity."
  • Slow starts and close games nag at the coverage. Opponents have scored on the opening drive in three games, and Atlanta is 0-3 in games decided by eight points or fewer.
  • The defense earned praise in the 49ers win, led by rookie linebacker Kendal Daniels, who went from a hospital stretcher to 11 tackles and two late takeaways in a week. He now carries the green dot with linebacker Divine Deablo out.

What the Tampa Bay media is saying about the Falcons

  • Respectful. Tampa's media treats Falcons running back Bijan Robinson as the problem to solve, noting he leads the NFL with 907 scrimmage yards, and Bucs nose tackle Vita Vea called it "a headache type of offense."
  • Bucs coach Todd Bowles praised Penix's arm and ball security, saying, "He's really not giving the ball away," while noting Atlanta's pass defense ranks 29th.
  • Tampa coverage also tempers the praise, calling Atlanta decent but not great and pointing to the Falcons falling behind Chicago 31-7 before a late rally.

What the Tampa Bay media is saying about the Buccaneers

  • Hopeful but wary. Tampa coverage calls the 43-13 win in Charlotte the most complete game of the season, built on 258 rushing yards and 10 sacks in two games.
  • Mayfield is the question. He wore a special glove on his throwing hand and threw 11 passes, and local writers doubt he could handle 30-plus throws and several hits. Sunday is framed as a no-excuses game for him.
  • The defense has become the identity: third in total defense, second against the run, and cornerback Jacob Parrish leads the NFL with 11 passes defensed. Safety Antoine Winfield Jr. returns from a rib fracture.
  • The 0-4 home record stings, with opposing fans taking over Raymond James Stadium, and Mayfield admitted, "We haven't won a home game yet."

What the Atlanta media is saying about the Buccaneers

  • Atlanta's media sees a dangerous team hiding behind a 2-5 record, citing the NFC South's only positive point differential, an eighth-place DVOA ranking, and the best division playoff odds of any NFC South team.
  • The Bucs' run game and blitz-heavy defense are the main worries. Tampa blitzes 38% of the time, fourth-most in the NFL, and Bucs edge rusher David Walker leads the league in pass-rush win rate.
  • The doubts center on Mayfield's health and a passing game that ranks last in the league.

What everyone else is saying about this game

  • Falcons: National coverage calls the Falcons one of the hardest teams in the league to read, able to beat or lose to anyone, though Penix's growth is drawing real attention.
  • Falcons: The Falcons have not had a winning season or made the playoffs since 2017, and national writers frame Penix's run as Atlanta's best shot at a franchise quarterback since Matt Ryan.
  • Buccaneers: The Bucs are seen as possibly overvalued after one big win, with Mayfield's thumb and Buccaneers wide receiver Emeka Egbuka's absence capping the offense.
  • Buccaneers: The Mayfield and Stefanski reunion drew national attention, with Mayfield's short answers read as motivation and Stefanski calling him "still a playmaker."

Who's out

  • Out: Falcons running back Brian Robinson Jr. (hamstring).
  • Out: Falcons linebacker Divine Deablo (forearm) and defensive back Billy Bowman Jr. (hamstring). Both were downgraded from questionable on Saturday.
  • Out: Falcons wide receiver Beaux Collins (hip) and offensive tackle Cameron Williams (ankle). Both remain on injured reserve within their practice windows.
  • Questionable: Falcons defensive ends Samson Ebukam (hamstring) and Charles Snowden (back), cornerback Avieon Terrell (illness), and wide receiver Olamide Zaccheaus (shoulder).
  • Out: Buccaneers wide receiver Emeka Egbuka (foot). He cleared concussion protocol but the foot injury keeps him out.
  • Questionable: Buccaneers running back Kenneth Gainwell (hip) and tackle Luke Goedeke (calf).
  • In: Buccaneers quarterback Baker Mayfield (right thumb) and safety Antoine Winfield Jr. (rib) practiced fully and carry no game status.
