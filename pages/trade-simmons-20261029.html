Bears Go All-In for Jeffery Simmons as Titans Restart the Rebuild

Chicago sent two first-round picks to Tennessee for the All-Pro defensive tackle, and each side got exactly what it wanted.

Chicago coverage is thrilled. The trade reads as the moment the Bears stopped rebuilding and started chasing a Super Bowl, and even the cautious pieces mostly worry about the cost, not the player. National coverage likes the Bears' ambition but keeps asking whether they paid too much, while Tennessee's media treats Chicago as a contender that bought a star. In Nashville the mood is raw: fans and local TV and radio are angry and tired of rebuilding, the team site and some local analysts back the return, and national writers mostly call the trade a good haul for a 1-6 team while doubting the franchise's direction.

The Summary

  • The terms: the Bears get Titans defensive tackle Jeffery Simmons and a 2028 fifth-round pick. The Titans get Chicago's 2027 first-round pick, 2028 first-round pick, and 2027 fourth-round pick. The teams agreed Oct. 29, pending a physical.
  • The contract: Simmons, 29, is signed through 2030 on a three-year, $105.8 million extension he signed in June with $100 million guaranteed.
  • Why Chicago did it: the Bears are 5-2 behind Caleb Williams, their defensive coordinator confirmed Super Bowl teams have a "red-level" interior lineman, and the team was "all-in" from the start of training camp.
  • Why Tennessee did it: the Titans are 1-6 after back-to-back 3-14 seasons. GM Mike Borgonzi said he never shopped Simmons until Poles called late Wednesday night, and called the offer "too good to pass up."
  • What's next: Simmons debuted Monday night at Seattle. The trade deadline is Nov. 10.

What the Chicago Media Is Saying About the Bears

  • Fired up. Chicago coverage calls this the end of Poles' rebuild and the start of a real Super Bowl chase. Bears defensive coordinator Dennis Allen compared it to "waking up on Christmas morning."
  • The fit is the story. Simmons fixes a weak interior pass rush, should draw double-teams that free Bears defensive end Montez Sweat and Grady Jarrett, and reunites with Sweat from Mississippi State.
  • The worry is the price. Chicago keeps comparing this to the 2018 Khalil Mack trade, which produced two playoff games and two losses, and points out that the Bears won't pick in the first round again until 2029.

Win-Win or Fleeced?

The grades lean toward Tennessee: the Titans mostly land in the A range for turning a 29-year-old into two firsts during a 1-6 season, while the Bears mostly get B-range marks for paying a steep price. Still, nobody calls it a fleecing; Chicago got a star, Tennessee got the picks, and the grades read as a win-win with the risk on the Bears.
