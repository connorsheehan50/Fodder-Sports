Jordan Love threw three touchdowns and Green Bay's pass rush took over, but the run game stalled again, and Carolina fired its defensive coordinator the next morning.

The summary

  • Green Bay won 27-19 at Lambeau Field and moved to 5-3. Carolina fell to 3-4, its first back-to-back losses this season, but it is still in the race for first place in the NFC South.
  • Packers quarterback Jordan Love went 30 of 41 for 288 yards, three touchdowns, and one interception, and he was not sacked. Packers receiver Christian Watson caught eight passes for 113 yards after a trip to the hospital with a broken rib.
  • Green Bay's defense sacked Panthers quarterback Bryce Young six times and hit him nine times. Young finished 23 of 41 for 209 yards, one touchdown, and one interception behind a line missing both starting tackles and its left guard.
  • Green Bay led 27-10 in the third quarter, then stalled. Carolina outgained the Packers 109-32 in the fourth quarter and got within 27-19 on Panthers receiver Jalen Coker's 26-yard touchdown, but the two-point try failed and the defense stopped a fourth-and-5 with 2:41 left.
  • The Packers' run game failed again against the league's worst run defense: 66 yards on 26 carries, with Packers running back Josh Jacobs at 39 yards on 21 carries. "We gotta figure it out, because it's not good enough," Packers coach Matt LaFleur said.
  • On Friday, Carolina fired defensive coordinator Ejiro Evero and defensive passing game coordinator Jonathan Cooley. Defensive line coach Todd Wash is the interim coordinator and will call the defense.

What the Carolina media is saying about the Panthers

  • Fed up. Charlotte coverage calls this another bad night and blames the offensive line first: Panthers left tackle Ikem Ekwonu, right tackle Taylor Moton (fractured fibula, injured reserve), and guard Damien Lewis were all out, and Young has now been sacked 11 times in two games.
  • The pass rush and the secondary are the other worries. Carolina had zero sacks and zero quarterback hits with pass rusher Jaelan Phillips out, and cornerback Will Lee III's hamstring injury left the team with almost no healthy corners.
  • There are bright spots. Panthers running back Chuba Hubbard had 119 yards from scrimmage, the run defense held Green Bay to 2.5 yards per carry, and rookie safety Zakee Wheatley got his first interception.
  • The mood is close to giving up on the season but not there yet. Charlotte's media notes the defense forced three turnovers and got only three points from them, and it read the Evero firing as a response to a defense that has failed all year.

What the Green Bay media is saying about the Panthers

  • Green Bay coverage saw a Carolina offense that never got going: 87 yards in the first half, 3.7 yards per play, and 5 of 18 on third down. Coker's touchdown was the Panthers' only play longer than 20 yards.
  • It credits Carolina's front for slowing the run game, but it notes the Panthers were missing their best pass rusher and called their run defense the worst in the league coming in.
  • Young was effective when he had time and lost when he didn't. Green Bay coverage points out he was pressured on more than half his dropbacks.

What the Green Bay media is saying about the Packers

  • Relieved. Green Bay coverage calls this an unsatisfying win: an opening-kickoff fumble by returner Coleman Owen, a missed extra point, and a fumble by Watson, all in the first 17 minutes, then a shaky fourth quarter.
  • Love is the reason it held. Green Bay's media calls him elite and says the offense is leaning on him too much, and Packers tight end Tucker Kraft (six catches, 53 yards, touchdown) and receiver Matthew Golden (seven catches, 79 yards, touchdown) helped carry it.
  • The defense is the excitement. Packers linebacker Ty'Ron Hopper had a sack, an interception, three passes defensed, and the game ball, and rookie pass rusher Dani Dennis-Sutton ended the game with a sack.
  • The worries are the run game, the drops, and the kicking. Packers kicker Trey Smack leads the league with eight missed kicks, and Green Bay coverage wants to know why guard Kevin Zeitler stayed on the bench while the line kept changing.

What the Carolina media is saying about the Packers

  • Respect. Charlotte coverage credits Green Bay's stunts, four-man rushes, and overloaded fronts for six sacks and nine hits. "They schemed us up a little bit," Panthers guard Robert Hunt said.
  • It says Love picked apart a depleted secondary, with a wide-open Golden touchdown just before halftime, even though Carolina held the Packers' run game in check.
  • "They were better than us today, for sure. They deserve that win," Young said.

What everyone else is saying

  • Packers: National coverage is the most excited about Love, calling him an MVP candidate whose value grows as the run game fails. He is the first quarterback to pass 2,000 yards this season.
  • Packers: Watson's return from the hospital was the national story of the night. "It was super dramatic," Watson said, and safety Xavier McKinney said it "shows the love that he has for this team."
  • Packers: National analysis also flags the run game as a real problem: Green Bay ranks last in rushing efficiency, and LaFleur said he has little confidence it will improve soon.
  • Panthers: National coverage focuses on Carolina's protection and on Evero's firing. Under Evero since 2023, Carolina ranks 30th in points allowed and last against the run.
  • Panthers: Some national coverage has started asking whether Carolina should stay committed to Young, who went 7 of 17 for 41 yards under pressure Thursday.

Checking back in on pre-game coverage

The pregame coverage was right about the pass rush. Previews said Young needed to get the ball out fast against Green Bay's pressure, and he couldn't: six sacks, nine hits, and pressure on almost half his dropbacks. Packers pass rusher Lukas Van Ness had one of the six sacks. Most of the damage came from the depth of the rotation, with Hopper and Dennis-Sutton adding sacks of their own.

It was wrong about the run game. Green Bay coverage saw Carolina's league-worst run defense as the chance to finally fix the Packers' ground attack, and it got worse instead: 66 yards on 26 carries, and LaFleur called it "not good enough." Golden, questionable all week with shoulder and foot injuries, played and scored. Panthers receiver Tetairoa McMillan, one of the players Green Bay previews said to watch, was held to six catches for 58 yards.

The moods mostly held. Before the game, Green Bay's media was still stewing over the Detroit loss while Carolina's media was warmer on the Packers, and that is still true after a win that felt sloppier to Green Bay than to anyone else. Carolina's neutral pregame mood has turned sharply negative after the injuries, the protection breakdown, and a coordinator firing.
