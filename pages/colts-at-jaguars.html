The summary

  • The Colts (2-5) traded guard Quenton Nelson to Seattle for a 2027 third-round pick and a 2028 fifth on Oct. 27, then sent defensive tackle DeForest Buckner back to San Francisco for a 2027 fourth. Both were six-time captains on expiring contracts.
  • The trades came a week after owner Carlie Irsay-Gordon fired general manager Chris Ballard. Interim GM Ed Dodds now runs personnel, and Indianapolis does not own its 2027 first-round pick, which went to the Jets in the Sauce Gardner trade.
  • More moves may follow before the Nov. 10 deadline. Colts wide receiver Josh Downs has asked for a trade, and running back Jonathan Taylor, who leads the NFL with 8 rushing touchdowns, says he has not.
  • Colts rookie guard Jalen Farmer makes his first start at left guard in Nelson's place after three offensive snaps all season, and defensive tackle Adetomiwa Adebawore, who leads the Colts with 4 sacks, takes over for Buckner.
  • The Jaguars (4-2) lead the AFC South by 2.5 games after a 34-31 loss to Houston in London and a bye. Jaguars quarterback Trevor Lawrence has 14 touchdown passes and four interceptions in six games.
  • Indianapolis has lost three straight and 11 straight games in Jacksonville. The Jaguars lose safety Antonio Johnson (ankle), and the Colts are without wide receiver Anthony Gould and cornerback Justin Walley.

What the Indianapolis media is saying about the Colts

  • Deflated. Indianapolis coverage calls the trades a white flag on 2026 and the formal end of the Ballard era, with no playoff trip since 2020 and no division title since 2014.
  • The locker room is hurting but says it is still playing. Steichen called the departed pair "the definition of the standard," and Taylor said, "We're going out there trying to win."
  • The heat is on Steichen and Jones. Indianapolis's media wonders if a loss in Jacksonville could be Steichen's last game, and it questions Jones's 7 interceptions and the offense's turnover rate.
  • The bright side is youth. Farmer, Adebawore, and rookie Colts wide receiver Deion Burks are pitched as the core worth watching, and offensive line coach Tony Sparano Jr. said, "This is an opportunity for (Farmer) to begin his journey."

What the Jacksonville media is saying about the Colts

  • Jacksonville's media sees the Colts as a team in sell mode, calling them a mess and a fire sale that is punting on the season.
  • Jaguars coach Liam Coen pushed back on that idea: "It's two good players. We can't deny that." The Jaguars' own site warns that until a team gives up on a season it remains dangerous, pointing to the Colts' 16-10 loss to unbeaten Minnesota.
  • The local focus is on Jones's 10 turnovers in seven games and a defense expected to struggle without Buckner.

What the Jacksonville media is saying about the Jaguars

  • Confident. Jacksonville coverage sees a clear path to the AFC South title and a chance to build a three-game lead at midseason.
  • Lawrence is playing at a high level, and second-year two-way player Travis Hunter is described as the start of something special after scoring a touchdown and making an interception in the same game against Houston.
  • The worries are the run defense after the Houston loss, a pass rush that ranks 24th in sack rate, Jaguars wide receiver Brian Thomas Jr., who has not caught a pass since Week 4, and a thin secondary without Johnson.
  • Coen is guarding against a letdown: "This has to all be about our improvements, us getting back on track."

What the Indianapolis media is saying about the Jaguars

  • Indianapolis's media respects Jacksonville as the team to beat in the division, well coached, with the best quarterback in the AFC South.
  • It points to a Jaguars defense with seven interceptions in six games and the NFL's best run defense, allowing 3.2 yards per carry, as the obstacle for Taylor.
  • The opening it sees is pressure: both Jacksonville losses came in games where Lawrence was blitzed heavily.

What everyone else is saying about this game

  • Colts: National coverage is harsh on the Colts' direction, describing the trades as the end of a failed era, with no playoff win since 2018 and 12 losses in 14 games.
  • Colts: Trade grades were mixed: the Buckner deal drew praise for the cap space and pick it returned, while the Nelson return was called underwhelming for a three-time All-Pro.
  • Jaguars: Jacksonville is viewed as a strong team: 25.7 points per game, 17.3 allowed, and a league-best +31 points off turnovers, with Lawrence leading the NFL in touchdown rate.
  • Jaguars: The national concern for the Jaguars is the pass defense, which has allowed 320 passing yards per game over its last three games.
  • Colts: The Colts are No. 27 in our consensus power rankings, and the Jaguars are No. 6.

Who's out

  • Out: Colts wide receiver Anthony Gould (concussion) and cornerback Justin Walley (knee).
  • Questionable: Colts linebacker Bryce Boettcher (groin) and tight end Drew Ogletree (knee).
  • In: Colts cornerback Sauce Gardner (calf) and tight end Tyler Warren (groin). Both have no game status, and Steichen called them good to go.
  • Out: Jaguars safety Antonio Johnson (ankle). Left tackle Anton Harrison is on injured reserve with a torn ACL, and Walker Little replaces him.
  • Questionable: Jaguars wide receiver Jakobi Meyers (hamstring).
  • In: Jaguars cornerback Montaric Brown (hamstring) has no game status and is expected to play.
