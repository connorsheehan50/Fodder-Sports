Philadelphia coverage is grim: with Hurts out for the season and the defense collapsing, the market talks about selling at the deadline and questions Nick Sirianni's grip on the team. The summary

  • The Eagles are 2-5 and have lost five straight, their longest skid since 2016, after a 38-21 Monday night loss at Dallas. They won the Week 1 meeting 24-22.
  • Eagles quarterback Jalen Hurts is out for the season with blood clots, and quarterback Tanner McKee makes his third straight start. The NFL and the players' union are reviewing how the diagnosis was handled.
  • The Commanders are 1-4-1 coming off a bye. Quarterback Jayden Daniels is back from a left elbow injury but is 0-7-1 in his last eight starts.
  • Philadelphia's defense allowed 204 rushing yards to Dallas with no sacks, tackles for loss, or takeaways. It has eight sacks and three takeaways all season.
  • Washington allows touchdowns on 81.8% of red zone trips, worst in the NFL, but is plus-six in turnovers.
  • Both teams are short-handed: the Eagles are without wide receivers DeVonta Smith and Dontayvion Wicks, while the Commanders lost defensive tackles Daron Payne and Javon Kinlaw and are without wide receiver Terry McLaurin for the season. Eagles running back Saquon Barkley is expected to play through hamstring and back injuries.

What the Philadelphia media is saying about the Eagles

  • Gloomy. Philadelphia's media treats 2026 as a lost season and talks openly about selling at the Nov. 10 trade deadline, with cornerback Riq Woolen's name in rumors.
  • The defense is the biggest disappointment: Dallas ran for 204 yards, defensive coordinator Vic Fangio said he 'didn't have them ready,' and defensive tackle Jalen Carter called his own play 'trash.'
  • The offense ranks 31st in yards with McKee, a thin receiver group, and a backfield where five running backs have been hurt. Barkley playing is seen by some as a desperation move.
  • There are bright spots: rookie right tackle Markel Bell has not allowed a sack since his debut, and the locker room insists nobody is quitting.

What the Washington media is saying about the Eagles

  • Washington's media sees a vulnerable, wounded Eagles team and calls this the best chance the Commanders will get to save their season.
  • It points to the Eagles' second-half scoring drought (37 points after halftime in seven games) and a run defense that ranks last in EPA per rush.
  • Commanders cornerback Amik Robertson guaranteed a win, and the coverage frames a home loss to a 2-5 team as unthinkable.

What the Washington media is saying about the Commanders

  • Anxious. Washington coverage calls Sunday a must-win and a possible last straw for Quinn, with the team 6-16-1 since its 2024 NFC Championship run.
  • The worries are a defense allowing 28.7 points per game (31st), the league's worst third-down offense, and new injuries to defensive tackles Payne and Kinlaw, plus left tackle Laremy Tunsil's torn triceps.
  • Hope rests on Daniels, who has no turnovers this season, along with rookie linebacker Sonny Styles and rookie wide receiver Antonio Williams stepping in for McLaurin.
  • The bye brought changes: a simplified offense, Steve Wilks added to help with defensive game plans, and a push to play all four quarters.

What the Philadelphia media is saying about the Commanders

  • Philadelphia's media is dismissive of Washington, calling the roster thin around Daniels and the season a waste of his rookie-contract years.
  • It sees openings in a pass defense that ranks 28th, a red zone defense that ranks last, and a line missing Tunsil.
  • It still respects Daniels as the one thing Washington has that the Eagles do not: a healthy franchise quarterback.

What everyone else is saying about this game

  • Eagles: National coverage calls the Eagles' defense one of the league's biggest letdowns, noting its first game without a sack, takeaway, or tackle for loss since 2012.
  • Eagles: Sirianni's handling of Barkley's return from a hamstring strain drew criticism, with questions about whether the trade deadline played a role.
  • Commanders: Nationally, Washington is seen as squandering Daniels' strong play, with Quinn's seat among the hottest in the league.
  • Commanders: Washington's run game, led by Daniels' 8.5 yards per carry, and its plus-six turnover margin are the strengths national previews highlight.

Who's out

  • Out for season: Eagles quarterback Jalen Hurts (blood clots). On injured reserve.
  • Out: Eagles wide receivers DeVonta Smith (hamstring), Dontayvion Wicks (ankle), and Britain Covey (knee), linebacker Jihaad Campbell (hamstring/quad), and cornerback Jonathan Jones (hamstring).
  • Questionable: Eagles cornerback Jakorian Bennett (knee). Must be activated from injured reserve to play.
  • In: Eagles running back Saquon Barkley (hamstring/back) has no game status and is expected to play.
  • Out for season: Commanders wide receiver Terry McLaurin (torn patellar tendon) and left tackle Laremy Tunsil (torn triceps). Both on injured reserve.
  • Out: Commanders defensive tackles Daron Payne (foot) and Javon Kinlaw (knee). Payne was downgraded Friday night and is reported to have a season-ending fracture.
  • Questionable: Commanders defensive tackle Jer'Zhan Newton (pectoral), tight end John Bates (shoulder), and wide receiver Treylon Burks (foot). Newton must be activated from injured reserve.

Game statuses from the teams' official injury reports, Friday, Oct. 30. Payne's downgrade to out was reported Friday night after Commanders.com listed him as questionable, and Bennett's questionable tag comes from the Friday report as relayed by Philadelphia outlets.
