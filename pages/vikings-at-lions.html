The summary

  • Minnesota (6-0) is the NFL's last unbeaten team and No. 1 in our consensus power rankings. Detroit (4-2) is No. 11, has won two straight, and beat Green Bay 20-17 on a walk-off field goal.
  • Strength meets strength. The Vikings allow a league-low 12.3 points per game and blitz about 70% of the time, by far the most in the NFL. Lions quarterback Jared Goff has 14 touchdowns, no interceptions, and a 123.3 passer rating against the blitz.
  • Weakness meets weakness. Minnesota ranks 29th in total offense, and Detroit allows a league-high 391 yards and 291.8 passing yards per game.
  • Vikings quarterback Kyler Murray has 812 passing yards, three touchdowns, and two interceptions in five games, and he is 0-3-1 against Detroit. Lions offensive coordinator Drew Petzing coached him for three years in Arizona.
  • Lions running back Jahmyr Gibbs (shoulder) and receiver Amon-Ra St. Brown (ankle) missed Wednesday's practice but have no game status. Safety Kerby Joseph is out. Vikings running back Aaron Jones Sr. (knee) has no status after a full practice Friday, and running back Jordan Mason ran for 68 yards in his return.
  • The trade deadline is Nov. 10. Minnesota is linked to Colts running back Jonathan Taylor, and its own coverage wants help at guard.

What the Minnesota media is saying about the Vikings

  • Proud, uneasy. Minnesota coverage celebrates the best start since 2009 and a defense it calls potentially historic. Linebacker Blake Cashman had 12 tackles and two sacks against the Colts, and safety Harrison Smith got his 40th career interception.
  • The offense is the worry. Murray has been inaccurate at times and rarely pushes the ball downfield, and receiver Justin Jefferson has 23 catches for 302 yards in five games. Murray: "At the end of the day, obviously everyone wants to give him the ball."
  • Local coverage wants a deadline move: a lead back, with Jones playing through a knee injury, and a guard, with Donovan Jackson lost and Will Fries struggling.
  • Coach Kevin O'Connell owned the slow offensive start against Indianapolis: "That totally ends up being on me." Jefferson pushed back on the doubt: "we're 6-0, we're winning games, and what we're doing right now is working."

What the Detroit media is saying about the Vikings

  • Detroit coverage respects the Vikings' defense, and so does coach Dan Campbell: "This defense is as good as it gets." It notes the base 3-4 look and an 85.2% blitz rate against the Colts.
  • Detroit's media openly asks whether the Vikings are for real. It points to an offense that ranks 30th in DVOA and last in first downs per play, and to Murray being sacked 18 times. Campbell: "They're problematic on offense, as we know."
  • The returner is a concern: Vikings receiver Myles Price has the NFL's only punt return touchdown this season and averages 30.7 yards per kick return.

What the Detroit media is saying about the Lions

  • Rolling. Detroit coverage sees a team stacking wins behind Goff, who has 14 touchdowns, no interceptions, and growing MVP talk. Campbell: "We are improving right now."
  • The defense got a lift. Safety Brian Branch returned from a torn Achilles with an interception, and Detroit held Green Bay to 60 rushing yards. Campbell: "I thought our defense really, really stepped up, and they were the big story of this game."
  • The worries are real: a pass defense that still ranks last by yards, two failed fourth-down tries against Green Bay, and center Cade Mays's role in two turnovers.
  • Health looks good. Gibbs said, "I just got dinged up a little bit, like a little shoulder thing but I'm good," and Joseph has started his return to practice.

What the Minnesota media is saying about the Lions

  • Minnesota coverage sees a surging Lions team with a scorching offense that averages about 30 points. It notes that Goff is elite from a clean pocket and much weaker under pressure, the core of the Vikings' plan.
  • Vikings coaches were full of praise. O'Connell: "They have a really good football team. It's going to take our best effort." Defensive coordinator Brian Flores on Goff: "This is a major challenge -- certainly keeping me up the last couple nights."
  • Minnesota's media also sees openings: Detroit sits near the bottom in passing yards allowed, Jefferson has averaged more than 100 receiving yards a game against the Lions in his career, and Campbell's fourth-down calls draw criticism.

What everyone else is saying about this game

  • Vikings: National coverage says Flores's defense is on pace to be one of the greatest ever, but notes that Minnesota's 125 points through six games tie for the second-fewest by a 6-0 team since 1950.
  • Vikings: National skepticism centers on Murray, who was 4-of-11 for 67 yards on downfield throws against the Colts. National voices still argue the defense can carry Minnesota to the NFC title game.
  • Lions: National coverage rates Goff among the league's best this season but called his Green Bay win sloppy and risky despite a clean box score.
  • Lions: Campbell's fourth-down aggression is a national talking point: Detroit is 8-of-13 on fourth down but ranks 31st in fourth-down decision value.
  • Lions: Lions edge rusher Aidan Hutchinson leads all edge defenders in pressures (42), a key piece of the national case for Detroit.

Who's out

  • Questionable: Vikings safety Jay Ward (knee), added to the report Friday.
  • In: Vikings running back Aaron Jones Sr. (knee), tackle Brian O'Neill (knee), guard Joe Huber (knee), and tight end Gavin Bartholomew (ankle) have no game status.
  • Out for season: Vikings guard Donovan Jackson.
  • Out: Lions safety Kerby Joseph (knee). He is in his practice window but has not been activated.
  • Questionable: Lions cornerback Roger McCreary (foot), added to the report Friday.
  • In: Lions running back Jahmyr Gibbs (shoulder), receiver Amon-Ra St. Brown (ankle), cornerback Ennis Rakestraw Jr. (ankle), and running back Jacob Saylors (neck) have no game status.

Game statuses come from the teams' official injury reports, Friday, Oct. 30.
