Baltimore gets its two-time MVP back after a historic second-half collapse, and Buffalo comes off its bye with the NFL's top-scoring offense and a leaky defense.

The summary

  • The Ravens (4-3) have lost two straight AFC North games and sit last in the division. They led Cincinnati 13-0 at halftime on Oct. 25 and lost 35-13, becoming the first team in 76 years to lead by 13 or more at the half and lose by 20 or more.
  • Ravens quarterback Lamar Jackson returns from a high left ankle sprain after missing three games. Baltimore went 3-1 in his starts and 1-2 without him, and he says the ankle is "bearable."
  • Baltimore owns the NFL's best first-half point differential (+65) and its worst after halftime (-70). The Ravens have not scored a second-half point in two straight games.
  • The Bills (5-1) come off their bye with the NFL's top-scoring offense at 32.8 points per game. Their only loss was 29-26 to New England in Week 4. Allen is 8-0 after a bye, and Buffalo has won its first game after the bye 11 straight seasons.
  • Buffalo's defense allows 28.5 points per game, third-most in the NFL, and has given up at least 29 in five of six games. Bills safety C.J. Gardner-Johnson is suspended, so safety Damar Hamlin starts.
  • Buffalo won the last two meetings by a combined three points, including a 41-40 comeback last season after trailing 40-25 in the fourth quarter. The Ravens have lost their last three games in Buffalo.

What the Baltimore media is saying about the Ravens

  • Frustrated. Baltimore coverage calls the Bengals collapse a "doom spiral" and says it is not too early for alarm, with the defense's 35-point second half called unacceptable no matter who played quarterback.
  • The flaws go past Jackson's absence. Baltimore's media lists the NFL's most offensive penalties (38), Ravens running back Derrick Henry going 2-for-9 on short-yardage runs, deep passes allowed at 12.8 yards per attempt, and a red-zone defense on pace for the worst mark in franchise history.
  • Leadership is the theme of the week. Ravens coach Jesse Minter said, "Right now, we're too much of a momentum team," Henry said, "The leaders have to lead," and players met after the loss on their own.
  • Jackson is the hope. He said, "The sense of urgency is extremely high," and Baltimore's media frames Buffalo as a reset game and a chance for the team's best win of the year.

What the Buffalo media is saying about the Ravens

  • Buffalo's media respects Jackson as one of the league's elite players. Bills coach Joe Brady said, "There's a reason he's one of the best players in the world," and Hamlin called him "one of one."
  • It is skeptical of the Ravens as a team, calling them slumping and pointing to a league-leading 17 blown double-digit second-half leads since 2020, two of them against Buffalo.
  • The worry is the matchup: Henry has averaged 103.9 rushing yards against the Bills, and Buffalo's coverage says Jackson's return makes a struggling Bills secondary's job much harder.

What the Buffalo media is saying about the Bills

  • Confident. Buffalo coverage sees a 5-1 team that used the bye to self-scout and get healthy, and Brady said the team should "not feel like everything's broken."
  • The offense is the pride: Allen leads the NFL with eight rushing touchdowns and is the subject of goal-line history, and Bills wide receiver Keon Coleman is catching about 80% of his targets with a team-best three touchdowns.
  • The worry is the defense. Bills defensive coordinator Jim Leonhard said, "We're not a very good defense at overcoming adversity in the form of penalties right now," after late collapses against the Rams and Raiders.
  • Health is the other storyline. Bills wide receiver DJ Moore (shoulder) and tight end Dalton Kincaid (chest) are questionable, defensive tackle Ed Oliver returns from a knee injury, and Gardner-Johnson's suspension leaves Hamlin in the lineup.

What the Baltimore media is saying about the Bills

  • Baltimore's media sees Buffalo's offense and Allen as the biggest threat on the schedule. Ravens defensive coordinator Anthony Weaver said, "It's a big play waiting to happen," when Allen runs.
  • It sees openings on defense: Buffalo ranks 31st in yards allowed (386.8 per game) and 26th on third down, and gives up 12 points per game in the fourth quarter.
  • It also flags ball security, with Allen at eight fumbles over two seasons and Bills running back James Cook at 10 since last season.

What everyone else is saying about this game

  • Ravens: National coverage piles on the Bengals collapse, framing Minter's late-game decisions and the blown leads as a consistency problem that goes beyond Jackson's absence.
  • Ravens: Jackson's return is the national headline, with Baltimore 76-35 in his starts since 2019 against 7-14 without him.
  • Bills: Allen draws praise as perhaps the best goal-line runner ever, scoring on 81.1% of his carries from the 1- or 2-yard line, while national coverage flags Buffalo's defense as a real weakness.
  • Bills: Bills defensive back Sam Franklin Jr. had his one-game suspension reduced to a fine and can play, with Brady publicly defending him.
  • Ravens: The Ravens are No. 16 in our consensus power rankings, and the Bills are No. 3.

Who's out

  • Out: Ravens guard Olaivavega Ioane (knee) and safety Jaylinn Hawkins (knee).
  • Questionable: Ravens running back Adam Randall (neck) and cornerback T.J. Tampa Jr. (knee). Tampa was activated from injured reserve on Oct. 31.
  • Out for season: Ravens tight end Durham Smythe.
  • In: Ravens quarterback Lamar Jackson (ankle) has no designation and will start after missing three games.
  • Questionable: Bills wide receiver DJ Moore (shoulder), tight end Dalton Kincaid (chest), cornerback Dee Alford (ankle), defensive lineman Greg Gaines (lower body), right tackle Spencer Brown (back), and outside linebacker TJ Parker (knee).
  • In: Bills defensive tackle Ed Oliver (knee) has no designation and is expected to play for the first time since Week 4.
