Bears Hold Off Maye and the Patriots on a Career-Long Santos Kick

Chicago never trailed, turned an end-zone interception into a touchdown drive before halftime, and answered a big night from Drake Maye at Soldier Field on Thursday.

The summary

  • The Bears beat the Patriots 30–24 to move to 5–2, ending New England's three-game winning streak.
  • The swing came late in the second quarter. With Chicago up 13–7 and New England at the Bears' 15, Bears linebacker D'Marco Jackson, starting for the injured T.J. Edwards, intercepted Maye in the end zone. The Bears then drove for a touchdown and a 20–7 lead.
  • Caleb Williams went 22 of 33 for 308 yards and two touchdowns with no turnovers, his second straight 300-yard game. Maye answered with 321 yards on 24 of 31 passing and three total touchdowns.
  • Bears receiver Luther Burden III caught all four of his targets for 130 yards and a touchdown, all before halftime. Chicago put up 405 yards against a defense that came in top six in the league in points and yards allowed.
  • New England's last real chance ended in a punt after a false start turned fourth-and-2 into fourth-and-7, with the Patriots down 27–21.
  • Ben Johnson first sent the punt team out, then changed his mind and sent out the field goal unit. Johnson said the Bears used the extra time while Patriots defensive tackle Christian Barmore was tended to on the field and felt confident in Bears kicker Cairo Santos, who said he wanted redemption after hitting the upright from 53. Santos made a career-long 58-yarder with 1:34 left.

What the Chicago media is saying about the Bears

  • Happy, with a little unease. The prevailing mood is that the Bears are good and keep finding ways to win close games, and that lapses like the 48-yard touchdown allowed before halftime are the part to clean up.
  • Williams gets the strongest praise. Chicago sees him growing into the quarterback Johnson envisioned, with Burden emerging as his favorite target.
  • The defense is the open question. It ranks last in the NFL in yards allowed per play but eighth in points allowed, and its takeaway pace is well behind last season's.

What the New England media is saying about the Bears

  • The film-based reviews give Chicago real credit. Johnson's scheme was hard to prepare for on a short week, and Williams completed 14 of 18 passes against zone coverage.
  • Most of the instant reaction, though, centers on how the Bears capitalized on New England's mistakes.

What the New England media is saying about the Patriots

  • Encouraged. A short-handed team rallied from 20–7 down on the road against a good opponent and had the ball late with a chance to win. Help is on the way as the roster gets healthier.
  • Running back Rhamondre Stevenson is the standout, with 93 receiving yards and a touchdown.
  • The fixes they name are penalties, missed tackles, and the end-zone interception, Maye's eighth of the season. Receiver A.J. Brown, still looking for his first Patriots touchdown, is the storyline to watch.

What the Chicago media is saying about the Patriots

  • Chicago is sold on Maye. One Bears beat writer called it "one of the best games of his career."
  • Maye, more than any scheme, gets the credit for keeping New England in it against the Bears defense.

What everyone else is saying about this game

  • Bears: Williams's late throws, including a third-and-9 strike to Rome Odunze, set up the winning kick. Next Gen Stats gave the 58-yarder about a 28% chance.
  • Bears: Burden owned the first half, and Odunze took over after halftime.
  • Patriots: Maye played well enough to win. Two early drives that came up empty, a fourth-down sack on the opening possession and the end-zone interception, made the difference.
  • Patriots: The receivers had a quiet night, combining for seven catches behind New England's 10th offensive line combination of the season.

Checking back in on pre-game coverage

Leading up to the game, both Chicago and New England coverage expected a Bears win and pointed to New England's patched offensive line as the matchup to watch. Both were right. Chicago sacked Maye twice and the line was flagged four times, though Maye beat several blitzes with quick throws.

The biggest disagreement was about Maye. Leading up to the game, Chicago coverage called him inconsistent, judging him on his first three weeks (six interceptions) rather than the three before this game (10 touchdowns, one interception). New England coverage said he was playing his best football. His 117.8 passer rating backed New England, while the interception that decided the half backed Chicago's turnover concern.

On Chicago's run game, New England coverage expected more from it than Chicago did. Chicago's read held up: without the injured Kyle Monangai, Bears running back D'Andre Swift carried 22 times for 78 yards. The Bears still controlled the clock, running 66 plays to New England's 50.

Chicago had its way through the air against a New England defense where other offenses had struggled, with 308 passing yards and 130 to Burden before halftime.
