The summary

  • The Chargers (0-6) are the NFL's only winless team, their worst start since 2000, and are coming off a Week 7 bye. They are last in our consensus power rankings, No. 32, and score a league-worst 15.0 points a game with 15 turnovers.
  • Chargers coach Jim Harbaugh kept Mike McDaniel as offensive coordinator and play-caller after a bye-week review, and the offense is expected to be simplified. Chargers quarterback Justin Herbert took the blame: "It's not his fault. It's our fault as an offense."
  • Herbert has five touchdowns, a league-high eight interceptions, and 21 sacks behind a line that has started four rookies. Chargers right tackle Joe Alt returns from a neck injury, and wide receivers Ladd McConkey and Tre' Harris are back.
  • The trade deadline is Nov. 10. The Chargers have reportedly shut down inquiries for veterans for now, but Chargers outside linebacker Khalil Mack, 35, and wide receiver Quentin Johnston are the names most often mentioned.
  • The Rams (3-4, No. 15 in our consensus power rankings) beat the Raiders 35-21 behind 192 rushing yards from running backs Kyren Williams and Blake Corum. Rams quarterback Matthew Stafford leads the NFL with 1,989 passing yards.
  • The Rams are short-handed on defense, with outside linebacker Myles Garrett missing a seventh straight game and defensive tackles Kobie Turner and Poona Ford out. The Chargers are without safety Derwin James Jr., who could land on injured reserve.

What the Chargers media is saying about the Chargers

  • Bleak. Chargers coverage largely treats the season as over and keeping McDaniel as a mistake, with comparisons to a tank job and pointed criticism of the changes to Herbert's throwing motion and the light use of running back Omarion Hampton.
  • Trade talk dominates. The market is debating moving Mack and Johnston, questioning whether waiting has already cost the team Mack's best landing spots, and even running the numbers on a Herbert trade, while calling that very unlikely.
  • The roster itself is under fire: more than half the original 53 has been on the injury report or injured reserve, and the interior line still starts rookies after center Tyler Biadasz's season-ending injury in camp.
  • The hope that remains is narrow and centers on the defense, which is sixth in third-down defense and second in pressure rate when blitzing, and on Alt and McConkey returning. Defensive coordinator Chris O'Leary said, "We're all desperate for a win."

What the Rams media is saying about the Chargers

  • Unimpressed but careful. Rams coverage points to the Chargers' NFL-worst 15 giveaways, the most sacks allowed since the start of last season, and Herbert throwing an interception in every game.
  • Rams coach Sean McVay said, "I really couldn't care less what the record says," and his staff praised a "big, physical run and hit defense" led by Mack.
  • Rams media warns the Chargers are rested after two weeks to prepare and share some of the Raiders' weaknesses against the run.

What the Rams media is saying about the Rams

  • Relieved. Rams coverage sees the offense rediscovering its identity in Las Vegas, with Stafford completing 79% of his passes and the red-zone touchdown rate climbing to 69% over the last three games.
  • The run game is the story: Williams and Corum each topped 90 yards, and running back Kyren Williams said, "This is exactly who we are."
  • The worries are a pass rush with only 10 sacks and an injury list that includes Garrett, wide receiver Davante Adams, and tight end Terrance Ferguson. Rams defensive end Aaron Donald, back from retirement, is still looking for his first sack.
  • The coverage stresses the Rams have not won back-to-back games this season, and wide receiver Puka Nacua said, "There was no exhale after coming back from Las Vegas."

What the Chargers media is saying about the Rams

  • Chargers media sees a Rams offense back in form, led by Nacua, who averages 97 receiving yards a game, and a run game that leads the league in man-blocking runs.
  • It also sees openings: the Rams rank 28th in third-and-medium efficiency, their pass rush is depleted, and Stafford has thrown six interceptions under pressure.
  • The overall framing is two underperforming, hobbled teams, with the Rams far closer to their preseason hopes.

What everyone else is saying about this game

  • Chargers: National coverage treats the Chargers as a bottom-tier team, with Herbert last in the league in several efficiency measures and the offense 32nd in expected points added per play.
  • Chargers: Keeping McDaniel drew mostly skeptical national reaction, with the Chargers' nine-game losing streak tied for the second-longest in franchise history.
  • Rams: The Rams are seen as clearly better on paper, ahead of the Chargers in nearly every analytics category, but national writers note they nearly let the Raiders game slip and have yet to play a complete game.
  • Rams: Turnovers and kicking are the national concerns for the Rams, with Stafford's seven interceptions and Rams kickers making 6 of 10 field goals.

Who's out

  • Out: Chargers safety Derwin James Jr. (hamstring). He had a rehab setback and could go on injured reserve.
  • Out: Chargers guard Kayode Awosika (fibula), defensive back Deane Leonard (concussion), and wide receiver Brenen Thompson (quadriceps, hamstring).
  • Questionable: Chargers cornerbacks Donte Jackson (hamstring) and Rodney Shelley (hamstring).
  • In: Chargers right tackle Joe Alt (neck) and wide receivers Ladd McConkey (concussion) and Tre' Harris (hamstring) return. Offensive tackle Rashawn Slater remains on injured reserve.
  • Doubtful: Rams long snapper Joe Cardona (ankle). Jake McQuaide would snap if he can't go.
  • Questionable: Rams offensive lineman Coleman Shelton (hip), tight end Colby Parkinson (rib), cornerback Trent McDuffie (groin), and wide receiver Jordan Whittington (back).
  • Out: Rams outside linebacker Myles Garrett (knee) and tight end Terrance Ferguson (ankle) remain on injured reserve, and wide receiver Davante Adams (hamstring) is out after surgery.
