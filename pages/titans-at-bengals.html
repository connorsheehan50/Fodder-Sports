The summary

  • The Titans (1-6) traded defensive tackle Jeffery Simmons and a 2028 fifth-round pick to Chicago on Oct. 29 for the Bears' 2027 and 2028 first-round picks and a 2027 fourth. Tennessee now holds four first-round picks over the next two drafts.
  • Tennessee lost 27-24 to Cleveland on a last-second field goal after leading 14-0. The Titans average 15.4 points per game, second-fewest in the NFL, and have gone 65 games without back-to-back wins.
  • Titans quarterback Cam Ward is 4-20 as a starter, with 1,179 yards, four touchdowns, and five interceptions this season. He has thrown an interception in five straight games.
  • The Bengals (4-2) scored 35 unanswered second-half points to beat Baltimore 35-13. Bengals quarterback Joe Burrow threw four touchdowns in that half and was named AFC Offensive Player of the Week.
  • Cincinnati leads the NFL in third-down offense and is tied for the league lead with 21 sacks. Tennessee allows 131.1 rushing yards per game and has lost its best interior pass rusher.
  • Tennessee is without wide receiver Carnell Tate, running back Tyjae Spears, and punter Tommy Townsend, which leaves running back Tony Pollard to carry the offense after his 129-yard, three-touchdown game against Cleveland.

What the Tennessee media is saying about the Titans

  • Gloomy. Nashville coverage describes a franchise at 13-55 since November 2022, 2-19 in its last 21 home games, and losing in the same ways week after week, with the second quarter (outscored 72-3) a running sore.
  • The Simmons trade is seen as the right call that still hurts. Titans coach Robert Saleh said, "It sucks. Everything about it sucks, unfortunately necessary," and general manager Mike Borgonzi called it "a really hard decision."
  • The bigger worry is whether the front office turns the picks into players, given a thin draft record and little so far from first-round defensive end Keldric Faulk.
  • Ward is the debate. He called his footwork "very good," and Tennessee's media questions that confidence, with some calling for backup Mitch Trubisky. Saleh said the trade "has nothing to do with Cam."

What the Cincinnati media is saying about the Titans

  • Cincinnati's media sees the Titans as badly weakened by the Simmons trade and a bleak injury report, saying Tennessee got much worse and has few ways to compete.
  • It is dismissive of Ward, calling him an overrated No. 1 pick, and points to a defense that allows 364.1 yards per game and has given up 100-yard games to three different receivers.
  • It still respects what Simmons meant. Bengals coach Zac Taylor said before the trade, "He's arguably up there with all of the top guys," and Cincinnati notes he sacked Burrow three times in the 2021 playoffs.

What the Cincinnati media is saying about the Bengals

  • Rolling. Cincinnati coverage sees a team that found itself in the second half at Baltimore, with Burrow throwing eight touchdowns over the last two games.
  • Bengals wide receivers Ja'Marr Chase and Tee Higgins are celebrated as one of the great tandems in NFL history. Higgins is on pace for about 1,600 yards and said, "We've built something special here."
  • The defensive moves look smart. Bengals defensive tackle Dexter Lawrence II and edge rusher Boye Mafe lead a pass rush tied for the NFL lead in sacks, and Cincinnati's media calls the Lawrence trade a masterstroke.
  • The worries are tight end depth after Erick All Jr.'s torn ACL and Tanner Hudson's season-ending pectoral injury, plus a third-down defense ranked 30th. Burrow called the tight end losses "two huge losses for us."

What the Tennessee media is saying about the Bengals

  • Tennessee's media sees Burrow as heating up at the worst possible time for a Titans pass defense that allows 69.2% completions, second-worst in the NFL.
  • It calls Cincinnati's offense one of the league's best, noting 30 or more points in each of the last two weeks and Chase and Higgins accounting for most of the Bengals' big plays.
  • The opening it sees is on the ground: the Bengals allow 116.3 rushing yards per game, and Tennessee wants Pollard to control the clock.

What everyone else is saying about this game

  • Titans: National coverage gives Tennessee high marks for the Simmons trade as a deliberate rebuilding move, with the return described as one of the best for an in-season trade in about 20 years.
  • Titans: Inside the building, Saleh told the team directly, saying, "We just traded Jeff," and the Titans responded with one of their best practices of the season.
  • Bengals: National coverage frames Cincinnati as the better team at nearly every position, led by Burrow's 110.4 passer rating and Lawrence's interior pass rush.
  • Bengals: Bengals defensive end Shemar Stewart, a 2025 first-round pick, was a healthy scratch in Baltimore, and Taylor shut down trade talk: "We really like our guys."
  • Titans: The Titans are No. 31 in our consensus power rankings, and the Bengals are No. 9.

Who's out

  • Out: Titans running back Tyjae Spears (knee), wide receiver Carnell Tate (shoulder), and punter Tommy Townsend (left knee).
  • Questionable: Titans defensive lineman John Franklin-Myers (groin, back), cornerback Jaylon Jones (knee), wide receiver Calvin Ridley (hip), and linebacker Dorian Mausi (foot). Mausi is in his return window and must be activated to play.
  • In: Titans running back Tony Pollard (rest) has no designation.
  • Out: Bengals safety Kyle Dugger (quadricep).
  • Questionable: Bengals linebacker Joe Giles-Harris (calf).
  • Out for season: Bengals tight end Erick All Jr. (knee, torn ACL), on injured reserve, and tight end Tanner Hudson (pectoral).
  • In: Bengals wide receiver Ja'Marr Chase (ankle) and safety Jordan Battle (hamstring) have no designation.

Game statuses are from the teams' official injury reports, Friday, Oct. 30. Season-ending tight end injuries are from Cincinnati's local coverage.
