Kansas City tries to shake off a 20-3 loss in Seattle, while Denver brings a three-game win streak and a perfect home record into the rematch.

The summary

  • The Chiefs (5-1) lost 20-3 at Seattle, scoring their fewest points and gaining their second-fewest yards in Chiefs quarterback Patrick Mahomes's 153 starts. They won the Week 1 meeting 31-10 at Arrowhead.
  • The Broncos (5-2) have won three straight and are 3-0 at home. A win would put Denver in first place in the AFC West and give Payton his 200th career win in his 300th regular-season game.
  • Chiefs wide receiver Rashee Rice returns from a hamstring injury that kept him out since Week 4. Kansas City receivers caught six passes for 39 yards at Seattle.
  • Kicking is a storyline in Kansas City. Chiefs kicker Harrison Butker is 11-for-16 on field goals, and the team signed kicker Justin Tucker to its practice squad. Chiefs coach Andy Reid said Butker will kick Sunday.
  • Denver's offense ranks 30th in yards (270 per game) and converts 31.1% of third downs, 31st in the league. Its defense is tied for the NFL lead with 21 sacks.
  • Chiefs running back Kenneth Walker III ran for 173 yards in Week 1, and Denver missed 19 tackles that day. Broncos cornerback Pat Surtain II returns after missing three games, while running back RJ Harvey and safety Talanoa Hufanga are out.

What the Kansas City media is saying about the Chiefs

  • Rattled, not panicking. Kansas City coverage treats the Seattle loss as a wake-up call: no red zone trips, 3-for-11 on third down, and Mahomes pressured on 19 of 36 dropbacks. Mahomes said, "If you don't come ready to play, you can get your tail kicked in this league."
  • The kicking situation is the loudest worry. Butker has missed 10 field goals and five extra points since the start of last season, and the Tucker signing is framed as real competition. Reid said, "Butker gets it. He knows that's part of the business."
  • The Simmons report, that Reid and Mahomes told the injured left tackle to leave a team meeting, drew attention as a rare leak. Mahomes said, "We've had the right conversations now. We're moving in the right direction."
  • The bright spots are Rice's return and Mahomes's 20-3 record in division road games. Local coverage expects the offense to look more like itself with Rice and a healthy Chiefs tight end Travis Kelce.

What the Denver media is saying about the Chiefs

  • Denver's media sees a Chiefs team with cracks: a kicker under pressure, a thin receiver room, and a pass rush it calls ineffective, with Chiefs defensive tackle Chris Jones and edge George Karlaftis managing little.
  • The fear is Walker. Denver coverage keeps returning to his 173-yard opener and the Broncos' tackling that day, and it expects Reid and Mahomes to be sharp after Seattle.
  • Broncos players speak about Kansas City with respect. Nix said, "They just know how to win. They built a great culture of winning."

What the Denver media is saying about the Broncos

  • Confident. Denver coverage sees a better team than the one blown out in Week 1: five wins in six games, an 11-1 regular-season home record since 2025, and the division lead within reach.
  • Nix's 222 passing yards and 62-yard touchdown run at Arizona eased weeks of criticism. Payton said, "he's our guy."
  • Payton's 200th win and the home crowd are big themes. Broncos outside linebacker Nik Bonitto said, "I think it's a huge advantage."
  • The worries are injuries and run defense. Harvey and Hufanga are out, wide receiver Jaylen Waddle is questionable, and the Broncos' run defense and tackling are the main concerns heading into the rematch.

What the Kansas City media is saying about the Broncos

  • Kansas City's media respects Denver's defense, which leads the league in sacks and has allowed 18.3 points per game over its last six games.
  • It doubts the offense: 30th in yards, 31st on third down, and only one of Denver's five wins by more than one possession.
  • The returns of Surtain and Broncos defensive end Jonathan Cooper are seen as real upgrades for Denver.

What everyone else is saying about this game

  • Chiefs: National coverage still rates Kansas City's defense and run game near the top of the league, but it flagged Mahomes's career-worst game at Seattle and Butker's misses.
  • Chiefs: The Simmons meeting dispute and the Tucker signing drew national attention, and coverage kept it factual while noting the controversy around both.
  • Broncos: Payton's run toward 200 wins is treated as a landmark, and national writers credit him with reviving the franchise in his fourth season.
  • Broncos: Nix's numbers are the national knock: 26th in QBR and 31st in air yards per attempt, even after his best game of the season in Arizona.
  • Broncos: Denver's defense gets steady praise, led by Bonitto's pass rush and Broncos linebacker Alex Singleton, who leads the NFL with 87 tackles.

Who's out

  • Out: Chiefs defensive tackle Omarr Norman-Lott (knee).
  • Questionable: Chiefs cornerbacks L'Jarius Sneed (knee) and Mansoor Delane (shoulder), and defensive end R Mason Thomas (personal, not injury). Reid said Thomas will meet the team in Denver.
  • In: Chiefs wide receiver Rashee Rice (hamstring/knee) carries no designation and returns after missing two games.
  • Out: Broncos running back RJ Harvey (knee) and safety Talanoa Hufanga (ankle).
  • Questionable: Broncos wide receiver Jaylen Waddle (chest), defensive lineman Zach Allen (hamstring), tackle Garett Bolles (hip), and safety Brandon Jones (hamstring).
  • In: Broncos cornerback Pat Surtain II (ankle) was a full participant and returns after missing three games.
