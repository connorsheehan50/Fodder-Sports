Bears Go All-In for Jeffery Simmons as Titans Restart the Rebuild

Chicago sent two first-round picks to Tennessee for the All-Pro defensive tackle, and each side got exactly what it wanted.

Chicago coverage is thrilled. The trade reads as the moment the Bears stopped rebuilding and started chasing a Super Bowl, and even the cautious pieces mostly worry about the cost, not the player. National coverage likes the Bears' ambition but keeps asking whether they paid too much, while Tennessee's media treats Chicago as a contender that bought a star. The summary

  • The terms: the Bears get Titans defensive tackle Jeffery Simmons and a 2028 fifth-round pick. The Titans get Chicago's 2027 first-round pick, 2028 first-round pick, and 2027 fourth-round pick. The teams agreed Oct. 29, pending a physical.
  • The contract: Simmons, 29, is signed through 2030 on a three-year, $105.8 million extension he signed in June with $100 million guaranteed. Chicago owes him about $7.9 million for the rest of 2026, with cap hits of $22.5 million in 2027, $30.9 million in 2028, and $29.1 million in 2029.
  • The history: Simmons is the first defensive tackle since 1967 traded for multiple first-round picks. He was a first-team All-Pro in 2025 with a career-high 11 sacks and 17 tackles for loss. This season he has four sacks and the best pass rush win rate among defensive tackles, 27%.
  • Why Chicago did it: the 5-2 Bears ranked 23rd in interior pass rush win rate, and quarterback Caleb Williams is still on his rookie deal. Bears GM Ryan Poles said Super Bowl teams have a "red-level" interior lineman and that the team was "all-in" from the start of training camp.
  • Why Tennessee did it: the Titans are 1-6 after back-to-back 3-14 seasons. GM Mike Borgonzi said he never shopped Simmons until Poles called late Wednesday night, and called the offer "too good to pass up." Tennessee now holds four first-round picks across the next two drafts.
  • What's next: Simmons can debut Monday night at Seattle. The Titans play at Cincinnati on Sunday without him, and the trade deadline is Nov. 10.

What the Chicago media is saying about the Bears

  • Fired up. Chicago coverage calls this the end of Poles' rebuild and the start of a real Super Bowl chase. Bears defensive coordinator Dennis Allen compared it to "waking up on Christmas morning."
  • The fit is the story. Simmons fixes a weak interior pass rush, should draw double-teams that free Bears defensive end Montez Sweat and Grady Jarrett, and reunites with Sweat from Mississippi State. Simmons is already acting like a leader, telling Bears linemen they have to win when he gets doubled.
  • The worry is the price. Chicago keeps comparing this to the 2018 Khalil Mack trade, which produced two playoff games and two losses, and points out that the Bears won't pick in the first round again until 2029, with Williams' extension coming.
  • Poles pushed back on the idea that Chicago no longer cares about picks, saying "That's really not what we're about" and calling Simmons "a multiplier" on the field and in the locker room.

What the Tennessee media is saying about the Bears

  • Tennessee's media sees Chicago as a contender that bought the best interior defensive lineman in the league.
  • The Bears' strength is the hook for Titans fans: if Chicago keeps winning, those firsts land in the high 20s, where starters are harder to find. Nashville coverage notes that an early Bears playoff exit would make the Titans' return even better.
  • The view on the price is measured. Tennessee coverage calls it steep but not a wild overpay.

What the Tennessee media is saying about the Titans

  • Angry. Nashville TV and radio heard from fans who called the trade an all-time low, said it stings more than the 2022 A.J. Brown trade, and asked "What is the plan?" Some blamed controlling owner Amy Adams Strunk.
  • The front office has defenders. Supporters of GM Mike Borgonzi call it a sober move: Simmons' prime no longer matches Tennessee's window, and four firsts in two drafts give options around Titans quarterback Cam Ward.
  • Doubters point to the Titans' record of 13-55 since Nov. 17, 2022, the leadership gap Simmons leaves, and how rarely late first-round picks become starters. A prominent player reportedly asked "Why did we do this?" in the team meeting.
  • On the field, the defensive line is suddenly thin. Titans defensive lineman John Franklin-Myers has 1.5 sacks this season and now becomes the top interior player. Titans coach Robert Saleh said, "It sucks. Everything about it sucks, unfortunately necessary."

What the Chicago media is saying about the Titans

  • Chicago coverage is respectful toward Tennessee's front office and dismissive of the roster. It describes Simmons as the best player on an "anonymous franchise" and says "The Titans' loss is the Bears' gain."
  • The Bears' side thanked Tennessee warmly. Poles said "I know this was not easy for [the Titans]," and Simmons said the organization and Strunk treated him well over eight seasons.

What everyone else is saying

  • Bears: National coverage loves the aggression but questions the price. The Bears are framed as an NFC favorite and a well-rounded contender, with the cost justified only by a deep playoff run.
  • Bears: National coverage keeps returning to the age question: Simmons turns 30 in the offseason, and two late firsts were close to the most anyone has paid for a defender.
  • Titans: National writers largely call it a smart return for a 1-6 team, with the 2027 and 2028 firsts giving Tennessee flexibility at quarterback and across a thin roster.
  • Titans: The skeptics focus on the franchise, not the haul: the Titans spent more than any team this past offseason, won 4 of 24 games under Borgonzi, and have a weak draft record that makes the picks a gamble.

Win-win or fleeced?

The grades lean toward Tennessee: the Titans mostly land in the A range for turning a 29-year-old into two firsts during a 1-6 season, while the Bears mostly get B-range marks for paying a steep price. Still, nobody calls it a fleecing; Chicago got a star, Tennessee got the picks, and the grades read as a win-win with the risk on the Bears.
