The summary

  • The #17-ranked Texans (4-6) host the #23-ranked Ravens (4-7), who have lost six straight, most recently 35-33 at Carolina on a field goal with 1:47 left. Both teams are trying to stay alive in the AFC wild-card race.
  • Baltimore is without quarterback Lamar Jackson, who has not finished a game since early October, and is 0-2 with Jacoby Brissett since trading for him. Its defense has allowed at least 33 points in four of its last five games.
  • Houston's defense is third in the league in sacks, and Texans edge rusher Will Anderson Jr., the league's sack leader, was named AFC Defensive Player of the Week after the 26-3 win over the Colts. Running back Woody Marks is expected back from a concussion.
  • Houston won 44-10 in Baltimore last season. DraftKings has the Texans favored by 6.5.

The word on the Ravens

  • Baltimore media has hit bottom. Local coverage calls it a nightmare season, with mistakes in all three phases, a defense that keeps giving up big leads, and a punt returner cut after muffing two punts at Carolina.
  • The coaching staff is taking heat, and taking the blame. On the game-winning drive at Carolina, Minter said, "Poor job by me. It's my responsibility to put us in the best position." Safety Kyle Hamilton disagreed: "It's up to the players to go out there and play well."
  • Houston's coverage of the Ravens expects the pass rush to feast. Texans coverage points to a Ravens line that has allowed 27 sacks against a Houston front that is third in the league in sacks.
  • Houston remembers last year. Texans writers recall the 44-10 win in Baltimore last season, Houston's first road win over the Ravens.

The word on the Texans

  • Houston media is relieved and looking up. Local coverage says the win over Indianapolis put the Texans back on the edge of the AFC playoff race, with a chance to climb after Pittsburgh's quarterback turmoil.
  • Anderson is the headline. Texans coverage asks whether Anderson can carry the team to the playoffs after his AFC Defensive Player of the Week award, with five tackles, four quarterback hits, and a safety against the Colts.
  • Baltimore coverage sees the Texans as a must-win. Ravens writers say their only path back into the race starts with beating Houston before the Week 13 bye.
  • Baltimore is looking inward. Ravens coverage says little about Houston beyond whether cornerback Marlon Humphrey will be back to face the Texans.

What the national media is saying

  • Ravens: National coverage is harsh on Baltimore, calling the Ravens' second-half failures unprecedented and Minter's job security an open question.
  • Ravens: National writers have moved on to the Ravens' 2027 needs and say the team refuses to admit the depth of its problems.
  • Texans: National coverage credits Anderson's leadership as much as his pass rush.
  • Texans: National writers like Houston's chances, with Marks back to pair with David Montgomery for the first time since Week 5.

Who's out

  • Did not practice: Ravens defensive tackles Calais Campbell and Nnamdi Madubuike and safety Jaylinn Hawkins.
  • Limited: Ravens receivers Rashod Bateman and Zay Flowers, running back Derrick Henry, linebacker Mike Green, defensive tackle Aeneas Peebles, and guard John Simpson.
  • Did not practice: Texans defensive end Danielle Hunter, defensive tackle Sheldon Rankins, receiver Kayshon Boutte, tight end Brevin Jordan, and guard Keylan Rutledge.
  • Limited: Texans receiver Marvin Harrison Jr., defensive end Logan Hall, and linebacker Jacob Hummel.
