The summary

  • The #18-ranked Cowboys (5-6) host the #26-ranked Eagles (3-7) four weeks after beating them 38-21 in Philadelphia, a game in which Dallas ran for 204 yards. Every NFC East team won in Week 11, and Dallas is chasing the division-leading Giants.
  • Dallas has scored 91 points in its last two games, and Dak Prescott has nine touchdown passes over his last eight quarters, a franchise record for that span. The defense remains the worry: it allowed 30 points to one-win Tennessee in Mike Pettine's first game calling plays.
  • Philadelphia beat Pittsburgh 19-16 to end its losing streak, with Joe Milton going 11 of 21 for 141 yards in his second start. The Eagles have 11 sacks in their last two games but are without linebacker Zack Baun (injured reserve) and traded defensive tackle Jordan Davis to Jacksonville.
  • Dallas is without tight end Jake Ferguson and guard Tyler Booker. DraftKings has the Cowboys favored by 8.5.

The word on the Eagles

  • Philadelphia media is relieved, not convinced. Coverage welcomed the end of the losing streak but still grades the offense among the league's worst, with Saquon Barkley's 38-yard run the only touchdown against Pittsburgh. The pass rush is the bright spot, with Byron Young getting two of the five sacks.
  • Cornerback is the worry. Riq Woolen was benched against Pittsburgh after missed tackles, and with Jonathan Jones out, he is likely back in the lineup against CeeDee Lamb, George Pickens, and Ryan Flournoy. Quinyon Mitchell is expected to play.
  • Dallas coverage of the Eagles focuses on Milton. Cowboys previews say the defense has to keep Milton, a former Cowboy, from escaping the pocket to extend plays.
  • Dallas sees the Eagles as a long shot. Cowboys coverage treats Philadelphia as a team trying to get back into the division race, now without Baun and Davis from the defense Dallas ran over in October.

The word on the Cowboys

  • Dallas media is thrilled with the offense. Coverage of the Tennessee win highlights touchdowns on the first six possessions, and Prescott said the Cowboys are "absolutely" in playoff mode with every team in the division winning last week.
  • The defense still worries everyone. Local writers credit better communication and five sacks in Pettine's first game, but say 30 points allowed to Tennessee and the steady penalties show the problems are far from fixed.
  • Philadelphia's coverage of the Cowboys sees a great offense and a bad defense. Eagles previews call this one of the league's worst offenses against one of its worst defenses, and note Dallas is without Booker, one of its best interior linemen.
  • Philadelphia respects the receivers. Eagles coverage notes Lamb, Pickens, and Flournoy combined for 13 catches, 201 yards, and three touchdowns in the October meeting.

What the national media is saying

  • Eagles: National coverage of the Eagles' win over Pittsburgh stayed critical of the offense, even in victory.
  • Eagles: National writers credited the Philadelphia defense for ending the losing streak.
  • Cowboys: National coverage praised the Dallas offense against Tennessee, with Lamb in the middle of a historic season.
  • Cowboys: National coverage also singled out an effort lapse by the Dallas defense in that game, and the league fined Lamb $23,882 for unnecessary roughness in the 49ers game.

Who's out

  • Out: Eagles linebacker Arnold Ebiketie (oblique) and cornerback Jonathan Jones (ankle).
  • Out: Cowboys guard Tyler Booker (ankle), tight end Jake Ferguson (knee, ankle), safety P.J. Locke (concussion), and receiver Jonathan Mingo (ankle).
  • Questionable: Cowboys cornerback Devin Moore (hip) and safety Jalen Thompson (hamstring).
