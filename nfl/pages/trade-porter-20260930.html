Cowboys Acquire Joey Porter Jr. from Steelers

Dallas made a late-September move at cornerback, landing the promising third-year Pittsburgh corner in exchange for future draft picks.

THE QUICK TAKE
  • Porter, 24, was Pittsburgh's first-round pick (49th overall) in 2023 and started from his first season, posting 14 pass breakups as a rookie.
  • Dallas gave up a 2028 second-round pick, a price that reflects Porter's youth and upside but that most analysts found slightly steep.
  • Pittsburgh received a future second and a sixth, good return for a young corner who had not yet cracked the top tier but carried real upside.
  • The Cowboys' secondary had been a liability in the early part of the season, and Porter's arrival addressed the most pressing concern.
  • This was an unusually early trade in the season, with both teams deciding to act in late September rather than waiting for the November deadline.
What Dallas Media Is Saying About the Dallas Cowboys

Dallas coverage was guardedly positive. Porter is a name player with real upside, and the Cowboys needed help at corner. The B- grade reflects the cost, a 2028 second is real draft capital, rather than any concern about the player himself.

Local analysts noted that Dallas has the roster right now to justify prioritizing the present over the future. The question is whether Porter reaches the level that justifies the price.

What Pittsburgh Media Is Saying About the Pittsburgh Steelers

Pittsburgh's coverage was more analytical than emotional. Porter is a fan favorite given his family ties to the organization, as his father was a Steelers legend, which made the trade more complex than the numbers alone suggest.

Analytically, getting a second-round pick for a third-year corner who had not yet fully broken out is solid return. The Steelers got fair value in a market that could have asked for more.

WHAT EVERYONE ELSE IS SAYING

National analysts were mildly positive on the transaction for both sides. Neither team made a decision that will be remembered as transformative, but both sides executed based on their current needs and situations.

WIN-WIN OR FLEECED?

A transaction where both sides did reasonable business. Dallas gets an ascending young corner for a price it can afford in its current win window. Pittsburgh gets a solid return for an asset it was willing to move. No complaints, no celebration.
