The summary

  • The #4-ranked Bears (8-2) visit the #7-ranked Lions (8-2) with the NFC's two longest active winning streaks, five for Chicago and six for Detroit. The winner leaves Ford Field alone in first place in the NFC North.
  • The Bears have not trailed in five straight games for the first time since 1985, a run that began when Caleb Williams returned from a hamstring injury. The offense has stalled after early leads in each of the last two wins, scoring one touchdown over the final three quarters against Tampa Bay and only a field goal in the second half against New Orleans.
  • Detroit is 6-0 at home and has allowed 14.4 points per game over its last five, despite losing linebacker Jack Campbell and end Aidan Hutchinson to injuries. Jahmyr Gibbs leads the league in rushing yards and rushing touchdowns.
  • Chicago is 0-2 in the division this season, and Ben Johnson lost both meetings with the Lions in his first year as Bears coach. DraftKings has the Bears as 1.5-point road favorites.

The word on the Bears

  • Chicago media likes the record more than the offense. Coverage credits a defense that had six sacks and a safety against New Orleans, but says the offense went stale after early leads in each of the last two games, and flags D'Andre Swift's second-half fumble as part of a recurring problem.
  • The Simmons trade is the story of the defense. Since Jeffery Simmons arrived from Tennessee on Oct. 29, the Bears have 13 sacks and five takeaways in three wins, with Simmons getting 3.0 sacks against the Saints.
  • Detroit's coverage of the Bears calls them the biggest test yet. Lions writers say Chicago brings the best offense Detroit's defense has seen during its streak, with Williams the kind of quarterback who extends plays and runs for big gains.
  • Detroit respects the whole roster. Dan Campbell called Williams "a heck of a player" and credited an offensive line that has stayed mostly healthy, plus a defense that has grown in its second year under Dennis Allen.

The word on the Lions

  • Detroit media sees a defense that turned around. Coverage points to the drop from 29.6 points allowed per game in the first five games to 14.4 in the last five, even without Campbell and Hutchinson, with safeties Brian Branch and Kerby Joseph back.
  • Local writers still feel overlooked. Coverage says a six-game winning streak, and 31 points on Tampa Bay's top-rated defense, still have not made Detroit anyone's favorite.
  • Chicago's coverage of the Lions starts with Gibbs and the speed. Bears previews call Detroit a really good offense, and defensive coordinator Dennis Allen said of Gibbs, "It's going to take all 11 to be able to stop him."
  • Chicago writers know the Lions have had their number. Bears coverage notes that Johnson and Williams have yet to beat Detroit, and that Chicago cannot afford to fall to 0-3 in the division.

What the national media is saying

  • The game: National coverage calls this one of the best games of the Thanksgiving slate, with first place in the NFC North on the line.
  • Bears: National writers say the Bears offense has looked ordinary and point to a run game that has sputtered for weeks.
  • Lions: National report cards called the Lions offense close to unstoppable against Tampa Bay.
  • Lions: National coverage pushes back on the idea that Detroit is only beating bad teams.

Who's out

  • Out: Bears cornerback Kyler Gordon (shoulder), safety Elijah Hicks (concussion), and running back Roschon Johnson (ankle).
  • Questionable: Bears linebacker D'Marco Jackson (elbow) and cornerback Tyrique Stevenson (groin).
  • Out: Lions defensive tackle Skyler Gill-Howard (knee) and cornerback Ennis Rakestraw Jr. (shoulder).
  • Questionable: Lions safety Chuck Clark (hamstring) and defensive end DJ Wonnum (back).
