The summary

  • The #8-ranked Bengals (7-3) host the #25-ranked Saints (3-7) after a 22-20 Monday night loss at Washington ended their five-game winning streak. Cincinnati still leads the AFC North by a game over Pittsburgh and Cleveland.
  • Joe Burrow leads the league's top passing offense with 29 touchdown passes and six interceptions, and receivers Ja'Marr Chase and Tee Higgins each have more than 860 yards.
  • New Orleans has lost two straight. Its defense has allowed 22 points or fewer in three straight games, but the offense keeps stalling, and head coach Kellen Moore's play calling is under fire, including from his own center.
  • DraftKings has the Bengals favored by 10. Defensive tackle Jonathan Allen and guard Dalton Risner did not practice Wednesday.

The word on the Saints

  • New Orleans media is fed up with the offense. Local coverage says quarterback Tyler Shough has looked like a shell of himself for two straight games, and center Will Sherman said, "A lot of calls, I think, were being given out a little too late."
  • The defense is the one bright spot. Local writers point to a secondary picking up steam and 10 sacks over the last three games. "I think we're definitely making progress," linebacker Kaden Elliss said.
  • Cincinnati media is looking past the Saints. Bengals coverage has said little about New Orleans, treating Sunday as the start of a three-game stretch that runs through Cleveland and Kansas City.
  • Cincinnati sees a game it should control. With the Bengals still a game up in the division, center Ted Karras put it simply: "Still control the division."

The word on the Bengals

  • Cincinnati media is stung but confident. Local coverage calls the Washington loss a lesson in winning ugly, and Burrow summed it up as "Just not good enough to win."
  • Local writers still see an elite team. Coverage highlights Burrow's MVP case, edge rusher Boye Mafe's three-sack night that gives him 9.5 on the season, and kicker Evan McPherson's one miss all year.
  • New Orleans' coverage of the Bengals sees one of the league's best offenses. Saints coverage notes Cincinnati averages 29.8 points per game, fourth in the league, and leads the league with 297.1 passing yards per game.
  • New Orleans frames it as best against best. Saints writers set up the game as the Bengals' passing attack against the best stretch the Saints' defense has played all season.

What the national media is saying

  • Saints: National critics say Moore's offensive failures are becoming impossible to defend, and his job status is now an open question.
  • Saints: National report cards say the Saints' offense is sinking while the defense gets better.
  • Bengals: National coverage has Burrow in a two-man MVP race with San Francisco's Brock Purdy.
  • Bengals: National writers say mistakes from the coin flip to the final minute cost Cincinnati in Washington, and that its edge rush is a problem that can't be ignored.

Who's out

  • Did not practice: Saints tight end Juwan Johnson.
  • Limited: Saints guard David Edwards, tackles Taliese Fuaga and Asim Richards, center Erik McCoy, and linebacker Pete Werner.
  • Did not practice: Bengals defensive tackle Jonathan Allen and guard Dalton Risner.
