Steelers Release Aaron Rodgers After He Rips Mike McCarthy

A day after Pittsburgh benched him, Rodgers said he was done with the Steelers and accused his coach of sleeping through meetings. The team cut him within the hour.

All three groups lean critical of the Steelers, and the gap between them is small. Pittsburgh's own coverage is the mildest, since part of it agrees the benching was overdue. National and other-market coverage is harsher on an organization that let a coach-quarterback feud spill into public.

Each bar shows the overall tone of that group's coverage toward the Steelers since the benching, scored article by article. It measures how the coverage feels about the team, not about Rodgers.

The summary

  • Late Tuesday the Steelers named Mason Rudolph their starter for Friday against Denver and made Aaron Rodgers inactive, a day after head coach Mike McCarthy publicly committed to him.
  • Rodgers said Wednesday on The Pat McAfee Show that he learned of the benching from tight end Pat Freiermuth, not McCarthy, and that he will not return: “That ship has sailed.” He said McCarthy snored through a quarterback meeting last week and broke the team down at halftime of Sunday's loss with “one, two, three Packers.” League sources corroborated his account.
  • The Steelers released Rodgers less than half an hour after the interview ended. McCarthy said the benching was his decision and that “there was an ultimatum made that was clearly out of his job description.” The organization fully supports McCarthy, according to reports.
  • Pittsburgh is 6-4 and one game ahead of New England for the AFC's last wild-card spot. Rudolph starts Friday at 3 p.m. ET against Denver.
  • Steelers coverage runs from calm to alarmed. Some Pittsburgh columns call the benching overdue and say the team is better off without a declining quarterback. Others focus on a frustrated locker room and a coach who may be losing it. National coverage is harsher on the organization, calling it dysfunctional, while a few former players back Rodgers' account.
  • Everyone else is more amused than sympathetic. Denver is planning for Rudolph, and Philadelphia's media is asking whether the Eagles ended Rodgers' career. Jets, Packers, and Bears coverage takes a last laugh, while Cleveland, Jacksonville, Houston, Cincinnati, and New England coverage sees an opening in the AFC race.

How it unfolded

  1. Mon, Nov. 23After the 19-16 loss to Philadelphia, head coach Mike McCarthy publicly commits to Rodgers as the starter.
  2. Tue, Nov. 24, 11 p.m.The Steelers announce Mason Rudolph will start Friday against Denver and Rodgers will be inactive. No reason is given.
  3. Wed, Nov. 25, morningRodgers is not at the team facility, and his absence is excused. Players are reported to be unhappy with the decision.
  4. Wed, Nov. 25, early afternoonOn The Pat McAfee Show, Rodgers says he is done in Pittsburgh and accuses McCarthy of sleeping in meetings.
  5. Wed, Nov. 25, afternoonLess than half an hour after the interview ends, the Steelers release Rodgers, sending him to waivers.
  6. Wed, Nov. 25, later afternoonMcCarthy says the benching was his call and the team tried repeatedly to reach Rodgers. Reports say the organization fully supports him.
