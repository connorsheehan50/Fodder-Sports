The summary

  • The #26-ranked Jets (3-8) visit the #31-ranked Dolphins (1-9), five weeks after Miami won 28-25 at MetLife Stadium. The Jets snapped a five-game losing streak last week with a 27-24 win over the Chargers.
  • Geno Smith threw three touchdown passes against the Chargers, and the Jets converted 81.8% of their third downs, a franchise record. Breece Hall and Braelon Allen combined for 161 rushing yards.
  • Miami has lost four straight since beating the Jets and starts Kyle McCord, who is completing 44.6% of his passes in two starts, with Malik Willis on injured reserve. The Dolphins' defense intercepted Josh Allen twice last week.
  • This is the Jets' last game before their bye, and DraftKings has them favored on the road.

The word on the Jets

  • New York media enjoyed the win but is split on what it means. Local coverage celebrated the end of the losing streak and the offense's best day in decades, while others argue that winning hurts the Jets' draft position.
  • Smith has earned new respect. Local writers push back on calls to bench him for rookie Cade Klubnik and debate whether he should return in 2027. Head coach Aaron Glenn remembers October: "This team came down to our building and punched us in the mouth."
  • Miami's coverage of the Jets expects stacked boxes. Dolphins writers expect the Jets to load the box against running back Ollie Gordon II, while noting New York is 30th in run defense.
  • Miami's staff picks lean Dolphins. Most Miami writers pick the Dolphins to sweep the season series, even with the Jets favored.

The word on the Dolphins

  • Miami media calls it a winnable game. Local coverage says the Dolphins have a real chance at a second win, even though a loss would help their draft position.
  • McCord is under the microscope. Local writers say McCord must be much better after a 12-of-33 day in Buffalo, though dropped passes and poor protection have not helped him.
  • New York's coverage of the Dolphins sees a dysfunctional team. Jets writers call Miami a one-win team whose offense ranks last in scoring, with a young defense that is fun to watch.
  • New York respects Miami's young defenders. Jets previews single out edge rusher Chop Robinson, who leads the team with five sacks, and rookie linebacker Jacob Rodriguez, who leads it in tackles.

What the national media is saying

  • Jets: National coverage praises Smith's steady play and the Jets' first win in more than a month.
  • Jets: National writers say the tanking debate after the Chargers win misses the bigger picture, and edge rusher David Bailey is the Jets' clear team MVP.
  • Dolphins: National coverage says Miami's offensive line is its most disappointing group and changes could be coming, starting with tackle Austin Jackson.
  • Dolphins: National writers see a rebuilding Dolphins team that competes but is not yet good enough to win.
