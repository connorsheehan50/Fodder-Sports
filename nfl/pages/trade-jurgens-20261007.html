Ravens Add Center Cam Jurgens from Eagles

Baltimore made an early-October offensive line acquisition, sending a fifth-round and second-round pick package to Philadelphia for the starting center.

THE QUICK TAKE
  • Jurgens, 25, took over as Philadelphia's starting center in 2023 after the Jason Kelce retirement and has been a reliable if unspectacular starter.
  • Baltimore gave up a 2028 second-round pick — a significant cost for a center — which some analysts found steep.
  • Philadelphia received a future second for Jurgens at age 25, which looks better over time if Baltimore's pick carries significant value.
  • The Ravens' offensive line had been dealing with injury attrition, and Jurgens provides immediate starting-caliber production.
  • This was the first of three offensive line transactions Baltimore would make in a five-week span, suggesting the front office was not satisfied with the existing depth.
What Baltimore Media Is Saying About the Baltimore Ravens

Baltimore coverage was supportive but noted the cost. A second-round pick for a center is a real commitment, and local analysts acknowledged that the Ravens paid a premium.

The prevailing local take: Jurgens is good, but is he worth a second-round pick? Most outlets landed on 'probably' with appropriate hedging given the subsequent quarterback situation that developed.

What Philadelphia Media Is Saying About the Philadelphia Eagles

Philadelphia received a future second-round pick for a 25-year-old center — but Jurgens' market value was lower than the pick suggests, in part because his play hadn't fully justified the expectations set when he inherited the starting job from Kelce.

Some Eagles coverage questioned whether the team undersold Jurgens. The prevailing sentiment was that it was a reasonable return but not a commanding one.

WHAT EVERYONE ELSE IS SAYING

National analysts were split on who won this trade. Philadelphia gets real future draft capital; Baltimore gets a starting center with youth and experience. The 2028 second is the key variable — if Baltimore is good, that pick is valuable to Philly.

WIN-WIN OR FLEECED?

Baltimore paid more than it should have but addressed a genuine need. Philadelphia received draft capital but perhaps could have done better. Neither side made a catastrophically bad decision, hence the modest grades.
