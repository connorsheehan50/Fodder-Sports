The summary

  • The #15-ranked Panthers (5-5) visit the #20-ranked Buccaneers (3-7) on Monday Night Football. Carolina leads the NFC South by two games, and a Tampa Bay win would pull it within a game with a season sweep for the tiebreaker.
  • Tampa Bay won the first meeting 43-13 in Charlotte, with five sacks of Bryce Young, two interceptions, and a fumble returned for a touchdown. The Bucs lead the league in rushing and in takeaways.
  • Carolina has won two straight. Running back Chuba Hubbard ran for 120 yards and two touchdowns against Baltimore, and he has 831 rushing yards and 11 total touchdowns this season.
  • Buccaneers quarterback Baker Mayfield is playing through thumb and foot injuries. DraftKings has Tampa Bay favored by 3.

The word on the Panthers

  • Carolina media enjoys the view from first place. Local coverage credits Young's late poise against Baltimore and rookie cornerback Will Lee III, and says the Panthers have learned to play with a lead.
  • Local writers say the trenches decide it. Panthers coverage says the path to control of the division runs through the lines, after Tampa Bay ran over Carolina in October.
  • Tampa Bay's coverage of the Panthers respects the change on defense. "We're facing a different coordinator, so schematically, it is different," Mayfield said. "Their guys are just playing fast."
  • Tampa Bay sees a chance to close the gap. Bucs coverage frames Monday as a chance to get within a game of first place and take the tiebreaker.

The word on the Buccaneers

  • Tampa Bay media is frustrated with Mayfield. Local coverage asks whether Mayfield has trust issues, and some compare his contract to the worst quarterback deals of recent years.
  • Local writers say there are no mulligans left. Head coach Todd Bowles said the team has no "mulligans" the rest of the way, and Mayfield isn't ready to give up on the season.
  • Carolina's coverage of the Buccaneers wants payback. Panthers writers say Carolina feels it owes Tampa Bay one after a 43-13 loss in which Mayfield completed only seven passes.
  • Carolina expects a heavy dose of the run. Panthers coverage says the Buccaneers' running backs carry the offense, as they did in the first meeting.

What the national media is saying

  • Panthers: National coverage praises Hubbard's season, putting him in territory once held by Christian McCaffrey.
  • Panthers: National writers call the Panthers' win over Baltimore one to remember.
  • Buccaneers: National coverage says Tampa Bay still has a problem at quarterback, with Mayfield and the passing game out of rhythm.
  • Buccaneers: National writers call Monday close to a must-win for the Buccaneers, and some question whether chasing the division does more harm than good.
