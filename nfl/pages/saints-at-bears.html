The summary

  • The #4-ranked Bears (7-2) come off their bye on a four-game winning streak that includes wins at Seattle and over Tampa Bay. They are 7-0 outside the NFC North but sit third in the division behind Detroit and Minnesota on tiebreakers.
  • The #25-ranked Saints (3-6) lost 20-17 to Carolina last Sunday when a 30-yard pass interference call on cornerback Kool-Aid McKinstry set up a 54-yard field goal as time expired. The loss cost New Orleans first place in the NFC South and dropped it to 1-5 at the Superdome, though it is still just one game out of first.
  • The run game is the matchup. New Orleans ran for 49 yards on 23 carries against Carolina and ranks 27th against the run (129.8 yards per game). Chicago ranks third in rushing at 142.0 yards per game and gets running back Kyle Monangai back for the first time since Oct. 4.
  • Chicago's defense allows 310.9 yards per game, seventh in the NFL, and opponents have converted just 22.8% of their third downs, the best mark in the league. The Bears also lead the league in time of possession at 34:31 per game.
  • New Orleans is thin up front. Right tackle Taliese Fuaga, center Erik McCoy, and tackle Asim Richards are out, and the Saints waived safety Justin Reid on Friday. Chicago is without cornerback Kyler Gordon (shoulder).
  • Chicago is an 11.5-point favorite, with a total of 48.5.

The word on the Saints

  • New Orleans media is fed up with the offense. Coverage hammers a run game that gained 24 yards on 17 designed runs against Carolina, and Saints running back Travis Etienne Jr. managed 14 yards on eight carries in his return from injured reserve.
  • Local analysts are focusing on Kellen Moore's job. In his 26 games as head coach, the Saints have trailed at some point in every one, and they have scored nine first-quarter points all season. The one bright spot is rookie wide receiver Jordyn Tyson, who caught 11 passes against Carolina, the most by a Saints rookie since 2007.
  • Chicago's coverage of the Saints respects the pass rush and little else. Defensive ends Carl Granderson (8.5 sacks) and Chase Young (8) both rank in the top 10, but Chicago's analysts see a soft interior line and a run defense that ranks 27th.
  • Chicago media also sees a patchwork offensive line. With Fuaga, McCoy, and Richards out, the expectation is that Bears defensive tackle Jeffery Simmons and a blitz-happy defense can take over the game.

The word on the Bears

  • Chicago media is all in on a contender. Bears quarterback Caleb Williams is described as knocking on the door of the elite tier, and four Bears made a national midseason All-Pro team: guard Joe Thuney and Simmons on the first team, right tackle Darnell Wright and linebacker Devin Bush on the second.
  • The worry is the run game. Since rushing for 291 yards in the opener at Carolina, the Bears have not topped 4.3 yards per carry in a game, and center Garrett Bradbury and running back D'Andre Swift are singled out for the protection problems. Head coach Ben Johnson spent the bye looking for fixes, and Monangai's return is seen as part of the answer.
  • New Orleans' coverage of the Bears sees one of the league's best defenses, run by former Saints head coach Dennis Allen. The third-down numbers are the headline, and New Orleans expects Allen's defense to lean on pressure against a line missing three starters.
  • New Orleans media also sees a ball-control offense. It describes Chicago as run-first despite Williams' rise, built to keep opposing offenses on the sideline, and notes the Saints must create pressure and turnovers to have a chance.

What the national media is saying

  • Bears: National coverage treats the Bears as a real contender. An AFC executive in a midseason league poll called Chicago 'the best-coached' team, and the Bears are favored to win the NFC North in that poll.
  • Bears: The national knock on Chicago is discipline: 72 penalties for 570 yards through nine games, and Johnson named penalties and turnover margin as the two things to clean up.
  • Saints: Moore shows up on national hot-seat lists. After the 1-4 start, owner Gayle Benson said, "I will not accept this level of performance as our standard," and the Saints face the Bears, Bengals, and Packers next.
  • Saints: Tyson is the national silver lining for New Orleans, and Saints great Drew Brees compared Shough to Bills quarterback Josh Allen. At 3-6, the Saints are still only one game out of first place in the NFL's worst division.

Who's out

  • Out: Saints right tackle Taliese Fuaga (knee), center Erik McCoy (biceps), tackle Asim Richards (knee), and cornerback Martin Emerson (concussion).
  • Out: Saints safety Justin Reid was waived Friday after missing practice for personal reasons. Safety Jordan Howden will start.
  • Questionable: Saints linebacker Pete Werner (hamstring) and cornerback Decamerion Richardson (thigh).
  • In: Saints running back Alvin Kamara and wide receiver Jordyn Tyson practiced fully and have no designation.
  • Out: Bears cornerback Kyler Gordon (shoulder).
  • Questionable: Bears wide receiver Zavion Thomas (ankle).
  • In: Bears running back Kyle Monangai (back after missing five games with thumb and turf toe injuries), wide receiver Luther Burden III, cornerback Malik Muhammad, and defensive tackle Jeffery Simmons have no designation.

  • The Saints lead the all-time regular-season series 18-14. The Bears lead the postseason series 2-1.

Team connections

  • Bears defensive coordinator Dennis Allen spent 10 seasons with the Saints: defensive coordinator from 2015 to 2021, then head coach from 2022 to 2024, when he went 18-25 and was fired midway through his third season.
  • Bears linebacker D'Marco Jackson, a Saints fifth-round pick in 2022, played three seasons in New Orleans. He has averaged 44% of Chicago's defensive snaps this season, though his last two games were almost all special teams.
  • Bears long snapper Scott Daly played in the Saints' Week 2 game this season before returning to Chicago, where he has handled the snaps since.
  • Bears defensive lineman Kentavius Street played for the Saints in 2022. He played in eight games for Chicago this season but is not on the active roster this week.
  • Bears defensive lineman Marcus Davenport was the Saints' first-round pick in 2018 (14th overall) and played five seasons in New Orleans. He has played in one game for Chicago this season.
  • No current Saints player has played for the Bears.
