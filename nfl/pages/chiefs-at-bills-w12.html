The summary

  • The #2-ranked Bills (9-1) have won six straight and hold the AFC's best record. The #3-ranked Chiefs (8-2) have won three straight, and the winner takes a big step toward the conference's top seed.
  • Buffalo has won five straight regular-season meetings. This is the 11th game between the two since 2020, including four playoff meetings, and the first Thanksgiving night game at the new Highmark Stadium.
  • Kansas City's defense allows 16.3 points per game, second in the league, and has held six opponents under 14 points, the most in the league. It also has the best red-zone defense, allowing touchdowns on 36% of trips.
  • Patrick Mahomes has thrown for at least 300 yards and three touchdowns in three of his last four games. Kansas City is without running back Kenneth Walker III again, and newly acquired receiver Josh Downs has given the passing game a lift.
  • Buffalo outgained Miami 405-185 and held the ball for 36:45 last week. Cornerback Christian Benford, tight end Dalton Kincaid, and guard O'Cyrus Torrence are all questionable.

The word on the Chiefs

  • Kansas City media is confident in the defense. Coverage points to a league-best six games holding opponents under 14 points and George Karlaftis' run of sacks as the reason the Chiefs can win in Buffalo.
  • Local analysts are asking about the schedule. Kansas City writers note that only one team the Chiefs have played, Denver, would be in the playoffs today, and some would be pleasantly surprised by a win in Buffalo.
  • Buffalo's coverage of the Chiefs is respectful. Bills previews note that since Mahomes became the starter in 2018, Kansas City leads the league in points and yards per game, and they describe one of the league's best defenses.
  • Buffalo media is focused on stopping Mahomes. Local pieces list the Bills defenders who have to deliver, with safety Cole Bishop among them, and the Chiefs playmakers who demand extra attention.

The word on the Bills

  • Buffalo media is all in on the moment. The locker room calls it the most anticipated game of the season, with center Connor McGovern saying "all eyes are on us" for the new stadium's first Thanksgiving night game.
  • Local analysts want a cleaner Allen. Coverage says Allen kept Miami in the game by putting the ball in harm's way, even as the Bills cruised.
  • Kansas City's coverage of the Bills sees the real test. Chiefs previews call Buffalo one of their toughest tests of the season and focus on whether the offensive line can hold up against an improved pass rush.
  • Kansas City media wants control late. Local keys to the game are controlling the fourth quarter, containing Allen, and turning Buffalo's mistakes into touchdowns.

What the national media is saying

  • The game: National coverage calls this the biggest game of the week, the latest chapter of Mahomes against Allen, a rivalry compared to the old Manning-Brady games, with the AFC's top seed at stake.
  • Bills: National coverage sees a chance for Buffalo to clear the Kansas City hurdle on the way to its first Super Bowl trip of the Allen era.
  • Bills: National writers point to the Bills' six-game winning streak and the AFC's best record as the bar Kansas City has to clear.
  • Chiefs: National coverage credits Mahomes' recent form and a defense that has carried Kansas City this season.

Who's out

  • Out: Chiefs running back Kenneth Walker III (groin), tackle Jaylon Moore (ankle), and defensive tackle Omarr Norman-Lott (knee).
  • Questionable: Chiefs tight end Noah Gray (rib) and defensive tackle Peter Woods (neck).
  • Questionable: Bills cornerback Christian Benford (groin), tight end Dalton Kincaid (thumb, ankle), defensive end T.J. Sanders (shoulder), guard O'Cyrus Torrence (ankle), and linebacker Dorian Williams (calf).
