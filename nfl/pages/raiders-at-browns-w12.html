The summary

  • The #14-ranked Browns (6-4) host the #21-ranked Raiders (4-7) on Sunday Night Football, their first Sunday night game in five years. Cleveland comes off its bye one game out of first in the AFC North and would be the AFC's sixth seed today.
  • Browns quarterback Deshaun Watson leads the league with six game-winning drives, most recently a comeback from 17-0 down to beat Houston 27-24, and Cleveland is 4-0 at home under first-year head coach Todd Monken.
  • Las Vegas has lost three straight and seven of eight. Rookie quarterback Fernando Mendoza, the No. 1 overall pick, threw for 267 yards and three touchdowns in a 38-24 loss at Denver, and tight end Brock Bowers is expected back.
  • DraftKings has the Browns favored by 4.5.

The word on the Raiders

  • Las Vegas media is focused on Mendoza. Local coverage says the season now comes down to Mendoza's progress, and the team site found hope in a locker room that watched him lead a comeback in Denver.
  • The defense is the problem. Local writers say defensive coordinator Rob Leonard has not met expectations, with a unit that struggles nearly every week and is not correcting its mistakes.
  • Cleveland coverage of the Raiders sees an edge for the Browns. Browns writers say Cleveland has a big edge coming off the bye against a Raiders team on its third road trip in four weeks.
  • Cleveland sees a rookie in for a test. Browns coverage expects a top-10 pass defense with 27 sacks to make life hard for Mendoza.

The word on the Browns

  • Cleveland media is enjoying the ride. Local coverage calls Monken a Coach of the Year candidate and says the flex to Sunday night shows how far the Browns have come from a 5-12 season.
  • Local writers credit the offense's growth. Browns coverage says the improved offense is the main reason Cleveland is headed toward a playoff spot, with rookie receivers Denzel Boston and KC Concepcion leading all rookies in yards and catches.
  • Las Vegas' coverage of the Browns sees a surging team. Raiders previews call Cleveland tough at home and in the fourth quarter, with a renovated defense led by Carson Schwesinger, Denzel Ward, and Mason Graham.
  • Las Vegas sees a run defense to attack. Raiders writers note the Browns have allowed 34 runs of 10 or more yards, a chance for Ashton Jeanty after a season-high 143 rushing yards in Denver.

What the national media is saying

  • Raiders: National coverage says the Raiders' loss in Denver was closer than the score and credits Mendoza's growth.
  • Raiders: National writers fault the Raiders' discipline after seven first-half penalties in Denver, and head coach Klint Kubiak called out the officials.
  • Browns: National coverage calls the Browns one of the season's big surprises and credits players who changed the outlook of the year.
  • Browns: National writers say the late wins cover up a problem: Watson and the offense need to start faster.

Who's out

  • Did not practice: Raiders defensive tackles Adam Butler and Tim Keenan III.
  • Limited: Raiders tight end Brock Bowers and defensive tackle Tonka Hemingway.
  • Did not practice: Browns guard Austin Barber, defensive ends Derek Barnett and Isaiah McGuire, and receiver Denzel Boston.
  • Limited: Browns tight ends Harold Fannin Jr. and Blake Whiteheart, center Elgton Jenkins, running back Raheim Sanders, and linebacker Quincy Williams.
