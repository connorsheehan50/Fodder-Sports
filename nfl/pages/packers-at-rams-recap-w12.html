Rams 26, Packers 13: Corum Runs Over Green Bay on Thanksgiving Eve

Blake Corum ran for 184 yards as the Rams held the ball for more than 40 minutes and shut out the Packers in the second half.

The biggest gap is on the Rams. Los Angeles coverage calls it the biggest win of the season and a sign the team can make a run. National coverage is warmer on the Rams than it has been all season, but it still hedges: some writers call this one of the strangest five-game streaks in recent memory, with confidence dropping even as the wins pile up.

The summary

  • The Rams beat the Packers 26-13 for their fifth straight win and moved to 7-4, into an NFC playoff spot for the first time this season. Green Bay fell to 6-5 and out of playoff position.
  • Tied 13-13 at halftime, the Rams opened the third quarter with a 14-play, 64-yard touchdown drive that took 8:02. When Packers linebacker Ty'Ron Hopper intercepted Rams quarterback Matthew Stafford on the next series, Packers quarterback Jordan Love gave it back two plays later.
  • Rams running back Blake Corum ran 32 times for a career-high 184 yards and a touchdown on his 26th birthday. Los Angeles ran for 241 yards and held the ball for 40:40, and Green Bay ran 16 plays in the second half without scoring.
  • Packers receiver Christian Watson left with a concussion late in the first half after a hit that got Rams safety Kamren Kinchens ejected. The Rams now hold the head-to-head tiebreaker, and Sean McVay beat Matt LaFleur for the first time in six tries.

The word on the Rams

  • Los Angeles media calls it a statement. Local coverage calls it the Rams' biggest win of the year and puts Corum at the center of it. Corum said McVay told the team the “most physical team” would win. “And I think we were that. I know we were that,” he said.
  • Local writers credit the defense as much as the run game. They point to three takeaways: a forced fumble by linebacker Nate Landman and interceptions by safety Jaylen McCollough and cornerback Emmanuel Forbes Jr. “Being able to stop the run was the biggest thing,” defensive lineman Braden Fiske said.
  • Green Bay's coverage of the Rams credits the defensive front. “That’s as good of a defensive front as you’re going to see in the National Football League,” LaFleur said, after Green Bay ran for 55 yards.
  • Green Bay media saw the Rams wear the defense down. Packers coverage says Corum ran more than 20 times in the second half as the defense tired, and LaFleur said of the run game, “We knew what they were going to do.” The Rams ran 71 plays to Green Bay's 44.

The word on the Packers

  • Green Bay media is alarmed. Local coverage says the playoff hopes are close to gone after an offense that managed 55 yards and two first downs in the second half. “It's extremely disappointing,” LaFleur said. “We've dug ourselves a hole.”
  • Parsons' return was the bright spot, and his words the story. Edge rusher Micah Parsons had a sack, two quarterback hits, and four pressures in 26 of 71 snaps in his first game in 346 days. “We need to figure out who's going to be a dog or not,” he said after the Rams ran 47 times.
  • Los Angeles coverage of the Packers saw an offense that disappeared. Rams writers say Green Bay never answered after the opening drive of the second half, with the Packers failing to mount a comeback once the Rams' defense took the ball away.
  • Los Angeles media noticed the Packers' miscues. Rams coverage points to Love's interception right after Hopper's pick, and a Packers line that let Rams defenders into the backfield all night.

What the national media is saying

  • Rams: National coverage calls it a statement win that should make the rest of the league take the Rams seriously again. Corum had 150 yards after contact, more than double his previous single-game high.
  • Rams: Some national writers now call the Rams Super Bowl contenders again after a 2-4 start, crediting a defense that has carried the streak. Others call it one of the strangest five-game streaks in recent memory.
  • Packers: National coverage is critical of Love, who has rarely looked settled over the last two games, and of an offense that fell apart once Watson left.
  • Packers: National writers say Green Bay's season is slipping away. One analytics model put the Packers' playoff chances at 19% after the loss, and Parsons said, “The only way to get out is, we probably have to win out.”

Checking back in on pregame coverage

What the pregame coverage got right: national writers said Corum was reshaping the Rams' backfield and that the run game would carry the offense, and it did. The Rams' defense came in having allowed the fewest touchdowns in the league over five games, and it allowed one more.

What it got wrong: most of the attention went to Parsons' return, which produced a sack but only 37% of the snaps because the Packers could not get off the field. Questions about Green Bay's offensive line were more on point than the Parsons hype, as the Packers ran for 55 yards.

Whether the moods held: they flipped. Green Bay coverage of the Packers went from leaning positive to critical, and Los Angeles coverage of the Rams went from neutral to positive. National coverage, close to neutral on both before the game, moved the same way.

How the score predictions did

  • The average of the confirmed score predictions was Rams 22, Packers 21, a near toss-up. The final was Rams 26, Packers 13, so the average missed the margin by 13 points and the total by 4.
  • No confirmed score picks turned up from either team's local media, so national picks are the only market.
